**The short version:** YouTube recommends videos people click, keep watching, and later say they were glad they watched. Recommendations drive more viewing than subscriptions or search, so this is the system to design for.

## What YouTube says it uses

From the platform:

- **Where recommendations appear:** the Homepage and the Up Next panel. Recommendations drive "a significant amount of the overall viewership," more than subscriptions or search.
- **Signals:** clicks, watch time, survey responses, shares, likes and dislikes. YouTube added watch time in 2012 because clicks alone rewarded misleading videos.
- **Valued watch time:** YouTube surveys viewers and counts time on videos they rate highly (4 or 5 stars) as more valuable.
- **No fixed formula:** how much each signal counts depends on the viewer.
- **Not your social graph:** YouTube says its recommendations don't rely on who you know, unlike social networks.
- **Search** ranks on relevance, engagement and quality.

## What to optimize for

<li><strong>Pay off the click</strong><span>The opening must deliver what the thumbnail promised, or viewers leave.</span></li>
<li><strong>Hold attention</strong><span>Keep each section earning the next minute of watch time.</span></li>
<li><strong>Leave them satisfied</strong><span>A video people are glad they watched is what the surveys measure.</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>Practitioner view, consistent with what YouTube says it measures</figcaption>
</figure>

- **Clicks without satisfaction backfire.** Since YouTube weighs watch time and surveys, a misleading thumbnail wins the click and loses the recommendation.
- **Think in viewers, not subscribers.** Because recommendations don't depend on your social graph, a new channel can reach people who have never heard of it.
- **Read retention graphs.** Where viewers drop is where the video stopped earning their time.

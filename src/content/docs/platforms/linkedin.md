**The short version:** LinkedIn's feed tries to match posts to each member's professional interests, and in 2026 it said it's actively fighting pods, comment bots and bait. It rarely pays you directly. It pays you in clients, jobs, speaking gigs and brand deals with companies that sell to professionals.

## What LinkedIn says it uses

From the platform, March 2026:

- **AI that reads the post.** The feed uses "Generative Recommenders," augmented with large language models, to understand what a post is actually about and how each member's interests change over time.
- **Your profile is a signal.** What members share about themselves (industry, skills, experience, location) and how they engage over time shape what they see.
- **Relevance over popularity.** LinkedIn says it wants posts that offer "genuine insight, actionable ideas, and thoughtful perspectives."
- **Crackdowns:** comment automation, engagement pods, and unauthorized third-party tools, which LinkedIn says it's working to make ineffective.
- **New members** are being asked to pick interests at sign-up, so the feed has something to match from day one.

LinkedIn's editors have also described the feed in interviews. Buffer collected those quotes (December 2025): LinkedIn says your connections and followers see your posts first, and knowledge-rich posts can travel beyond them. *(From the platform, via Buffer.)*

## What to optimize for

- **Write from what you've actually done.** Since ranking reads the post itself and favors expertise, a specific lesson from a real campaign beats a generic list. *(From the platform, applied.)*
- **Make your profile match your posts.** The feed uses profile data to understand who you are and who you're relevant to. *(From the platform, applied.)*
- **Drop the pods and the "comment YES" prompts.** LinkedIn named both as things it's working against. *(From the platform.)*
- **Reply to your comments.** Buffer's data says replying lifts engagement about 30%. *(Creator-tested, Buffer data.)*
- **Post a few times a week, not every hour.** Buffer found 2 to 5 posts a week works. *(Creator-tested, Buffer data.)*

- **Carousels (PDF documents)** for frameworks, breakdowns and before-and-afters. *(Creator-tested.)*
- **Short talking-head video** that matches its caption. LinkedIn called out videos that don't match their text. *(From the platform.)*
- **Newsletters** on LinkedIn, which notify subscribers when you publish. Good for a weekly "what I learned" from your field. *(Creator-tested.)*
- **Links:** Buffer's data shows posts with external links tend to get less engagement. LinkedIn hasn't said it penalizes them. *(Creator-tested.)*

## How you get paid

LinkedIn has no open ad-revenue program for creators. The money is indirect, and often bigger than on consumer apps, because the audience has budgets.

- **BrandLink.** Since April 2025, brands can sponsor video series from selected creators and publishers (LinkedIn named Steven Bartlett, Gary Vaynerchuk, Guy Raz and Allie K. Miller). It's invite-only and LinkedIn hasn't published how creators are paid. *(From the platform.)*
- **B2B brand deals.** Software, finance and recruiting brands pay creators to reach professionals. See [Brand deals](/earn/brand-deals/). *(Creator-tested.)*
- **Your own business.** Consulting, courses, a newsletter, speaking and hiring all come in through DMs. *(Creator-tested.)*

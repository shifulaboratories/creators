**The short version:** feeds show your post to a small group, watch what they do, and decide whether to show it to more people. So growth isn't a secret trick. It's a loop: make something a specific person wants, earn the first few seconds, deliver so they stay, give them a reason to send it or follow, then read what happened and change one thing next time.

<GrowLoop />

## Why it works this way

Every big platform now finds most new viewers through recommendations, not followers. Instagram says Reels and Explore are mostly accounts people don't follow yet. TikTok says follower count and past hits are not direct factors in For You, so every video gets its own shot. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

That's good news if you're small. It means the next post can find new people on its own merits. It's also why the levers below matter more than posting at "the best time" or using a magic number of hashtags. If you want the mechanics first, start with [how the algorithm picks what people see](/algorithms/how-recommenders-work/) and [what each platform optimizes for](/algorithms/what-to-optimize/).

## Three things that hold everywhere

- **The opening is a promise.** Packaging that wins the click and loses the viewer teaches the feed your posts disappoint. MrBeast's team plans the thumbnail first and then makes sure the opening delivers it. [Case study →](/case-studies/mrbeast/) <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
- **Original beats reposted.** Instagram, TikTok and YouTube all limit reach for unoriginal content and other apps' watermarks. Remake each piece for each feed. [How →](/grow/repurposing/) <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **Reps beat inspiration.** "Make 100 videos and improve one thing each video," as MrBeast put it at VidCon 2022. Buffer's data on 100,000+ accounts points the same way: people who posted most weeks got far more engagement per post than people who posted in bursts. [Cadence →](/grow/cadence/)

**The short version:** change one thing at a time, decide how you'll judge it before you post, and give it more than one post. Two platforms now have built-in tools for this: YouTube's Test & Compare for titles and thumbnails, and Instagram's Trial Reels for testing with non-followers first. Everywhere else, a simple paired test works.

## The built-in tools

### YouTube: Test & Compare

Upload up to three titles, thumbnails, or title-and-thumbnail combinations on a long video. YouTube shows each to a share of viewers and picks the winner by **watch time share**, not clicks. YouTube says it optimizes these tests "for overall watch time over other metrics, like click-through-rate," which is a strong hint about what it values. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

Good to know: it's in YouTube Studio on a computer, needs advanced features turned on, and doesn't work on Shorts, scheduled lives or premieres. If there's no clear winner, the first version you uploaded is kept.

### Instagram: Trial Reels

Flip on "Trial" before you share a Reel, and it goes to non-followers first. Your followers won't see it in Feed or the Reels tab, and it won't show on your grid unless you share it to everyone later. After about 24 hours you get views, likes, comments and shares. You can share it with followers in one tap, or let Instagram share it automatically if it performs well within 72 hours. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

Trial Reels are the closest thing to a free focus group. Use them to test a new topic or format without confusing the followers you already have.

### TikTok and everywhere else: the paired test

There's no built-in A/B tool for regular posts, so pair them. Post two versions of the same idea a few days apart, changing only one thing. Repeat over three or more pairs before you decide. One post is a coin flip, especially on a feed where, as Buffer's data shows, a few posts get most of the views.

## What's worth testing

In rough order of how much they usually move:

1. **The idea and the angle.** The biggest lever by far. Same topic, different angle.
2. **The hook.** First line and first frame. [Eight patterns to try →](/grow/hooks/)
3. **The packaging.** Title, thumbnail, cover.
4. **The format.** Talking head vs. voiceover, video vs. carousel.
5. **Length.** Usually tighter wins, but test it.
6. **Posting time.** Last. YouTube says total views usually even out over a few weeks regardless. [Why →](/grow/cadence/)

## Keep a log

Write every test down: what you changed, your guess, the result, and whether you'll keep it. After a few months you'll have something no blog post can give you: proof of what works for *your* audience. MrBeast's version of this was "make 100 videos and improve one thing each video."

**The short version:** repurposing works when you *remake* an idea for each feed, and backfires when you just *re-upload* a file. Instagram and TikTok both say reposted content and other apps' watermarks can keep a post out of recommendations. Keep your raw files, export clean, and re-cut the opening for each platform.

## The workflow

<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Make the master</strong><span>The fullest version: a long video, a detailed post or a podcast. Keep the raw project files.</span></li>
<li><strong>Find the moments</strong><span>Your retention graph's spikes are the parts people rewatched. Those are your clips.</span></li>
<li><strong>Re-hook each one</strong><span>A clip from minute 8 needs its own first second. Don't start mid-sentence with no context.</span></li>
<li><strong>Export clean</strong><span>From your editor, not downloaded from another app. No watermarks, native captions per platform.</span></li>
<li><strong>Rewrite for reading</strong><span>Carousels, threads and newsletters are written, not transcribed. One idea per slide or line.</span></li>
</ol>

## Don't post the same thing at the same time everywhere

Each feed rewards something slightly different. YouTube leans on watch time and satisfaction, TikTok on completion, Instagram on sends, X on replies. A joke that's built to be sent works on Instagram; a take built for replies works on X. [Compare the platforms side by side →](/algorithms/compare-platforms/)

Stagger posts by a day or two, and watch which version takes off. The winning opening on one platform is a strong guess for the next one. That's a free [experiment](/grow/experiments/).

<DeepDive title="What each platform says about reposting" hint="">

**Instagram.** Since April 2024, when two identical posts exist, Instagram only recommends the original. Accounts that post other people's content 10 or more times in 30 days without materially changing it are left out of recommendations until they stop for 30 days. Content needs "no visible watermarks" to be recommended. In April 2026 it extended this to photos and carousels, and TechCrunch reports it doesn't count adding a watermark or changing a video's speed as an edit. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

**TikTok.** Its guidelines say content is ineligible for For You if it's "unoriginal or reproduced content" without "new or creative changes," or carries someone else's visible watermark or logo. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

**YouTube.** Three-minute Shorts come with a catch for reused material: a Short over one minute with an active copyright claim is blocked worldwide. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

The good news, especially if you're reposting *your own* work: Instagram says it won't replace content that's "changed in a significant way," like re-edited, narrated with a new voiceover or remixed. Your own video, exported clean with a new opening and native captions, is original on every platform.

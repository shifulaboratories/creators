**The short version:** a format is a repeatable shape for your ideas: the same structure, a new topic each time. Formats are how one good post becomes a reason to follow. Pick formats that fit the feed you're posting in, and keep up with the rules that decide whether a post can be recommended at all.

## Turn your best post into a series

A one-off post asks a stranger to like *this* post. A series asks them to want the *next* one, which is what a follow is. That's why so many creators who break out do it with a named, numbered format: a challenge with stakes, a weekly ritual, a "rating every X" run.

## Match the format to the feed

| Format | Best at | Where it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short vertical video | Reaching people who don't follow you yet | Reels, TikTok, Shorts |
| Carousel | Saves and sends: steps, lists, guides, photo dumps | Instagram (up to 20 slides), also TikTok photo mode |
| Long video | Trust, depth, search, and income per viewer | YouTube |
| Text post or thread | Takes, conversation, replies | X, Threads, LinkedIn |
| Live | Your existing fans, community, support income | Any, once you have a core |

Instagram says Reels and Explore are mostly accounts people don't follow yet. Its head, Adam Mosseri, calls sends per reach one of the most important signals in ranking. Carousels, he's said, are a "deeper, richer format" that drives engagement beyond time spent.

Short video finds people. Long video and carousels keep them. Most growing creators use both: short to be discovered, a deeper format to become someone people follow on purpose.

A few things worth knowing from those rules:

- **A YouTube Short over 1 minute with an active copyright claim is blocked worldwide.** Under a minute, the old rules apply. If you use music in longer Shorts, use YouTube's own library.
- **Instagram's grid is now rectangular.** Old square covers get cropped. Check how your cover looks on your profile before posting.
- **"Unoriginal" is a reach problem, not just a copyright one.** Instagram and TikTok both say reposted content and other apps' watermarks make a post ineligible for recommendations. More on that in [one idea, five platforms](/grow/repurposing/).

## Don't make it longer just because you can

When Shorts and Reels went to three minutes, a lot of advice said "go longer." The platforms didn't say that. YouTube said it would be "working to improve recommendations for longer Shorts in the coming months." MrBeast's view on long-form applies here too: "I've never seen anything that says that videos need to be longer. Just make videos that people want to watch." Length should follow how long the idea stays interesting.

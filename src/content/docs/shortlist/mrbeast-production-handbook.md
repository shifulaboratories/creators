In September 2024, a 36-page internal onboarding document called **"How to Succeed in MrBeast Production"** spread widely on X. It's written for new members of MrBeast's production team and explains how the channel thinks about videos. None of the coverage we reviewed reports a comment from MrBeast or his company on the leak.

We don't host the document. Below is our summary of what published coverage reports it says, with links to that coverage.

## The takeaways

### 1. Start with the title and thumbnail

According to Simon Willison's notes, the process "starts with the title and thumbnail." Tubefilter reports the document treats them as "critical components." The document's own example compares a video called "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup" with a less extreme version of the same idea to show what earns a higher click-through rate.

### 2. The first minute has to match the thumbnail

If the video doesn't deliver what the packaging promised, viewers "click away," which drags down average view duration. Willison notes an example from the document where losing roughly 21 million of 60 million viewers in the first minute was described as reasonably good. The document also gives minutes 1 to 3, 3 to 6, and 6 to the end separate jobs.

### 3. Protect the "critical components"

"Critical components are the things that are essential to your video." The document tells staff to treat them like a "baby" and never leave one to a "coin flip," checking on them constantly instead of assuming.

### 4. Communicate in person when it matters

The document ranks in-person conversation above calls, texts and email. It says written communication doesn't count as communication unless the other person confirms they read it, and it asks staff to film their work so teams stay aligned.

### 5. Optimize for YouTube, and keep iterating

Fast Company's list of takeaways includes "Your goal here is to make the best YOUTUBE videos possible," "Creativity Saves Money," "Always be iterating," and "we are a results based company."

## Read it with context

- **It's one channel's playbook.** All three sources stress it's specific to MrBeast's format and scale, not a universal formula.
- **It has contradictions.** Tubefilter points out "grain of salt" caveats alongside rigid process rules.
- **Culture questions.** Coverage notes that lines like "No does not mean no" drew criticism, and places the leak alongside reporting on working conditions and the show *Beast Games*.

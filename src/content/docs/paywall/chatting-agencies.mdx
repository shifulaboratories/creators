**The short version:** on DM-driven fan platforms, a large share of income comes from paid messages. That has created a whole industry of agencies that run creators’ inboxes, staffed by “chatters” who write to fans in the creator’s voice. It can raise income, but it carries real costs: big commissions, loss of control, fans who feel deceived, and lawsuits.

Reuters found most agencies take 20% to 80% of a creator’s earnings, and some charge a flat fee. A 2026 BBC investigation into UK agencies found 50% of pre-tax earnings was common, with contracts reaching 70%. At 50%, an agency has to *double* what you’d sell yourself before you come out ahead. What the reporting describes:

- **Who the chatters are.** Reuters interviewed 15 chatters and found many working from the Philippines, lower-wage European countries and parts of the US, mostly writing as young European and American women. VICE found agencies outsourcing to Pakistan, India and the Philippines, with shifts of 4 to 12 hours and one chatter covering up to seven creators at once.
- **How they’re hired.** Job ads circulate on Reddit and in online groups. One Reuters reporter was put on a trial shift after a short phone call, with no apparent background check.
- **What they’re paid.** Often a small commission on what they sell. Reuters reported a top chatter could make up to $3,000 a month, while others earned $800 to $1,000. VICE heard of chatters making as little as £250 to £300 a month.
- **How they sell.** Shared scripts, nicknames for regular fans, pre-recorded content presented as new, and steering fans toward pay-per-view messages. Some use third-party software that keeps notes on each fan, and some log in with the creator’s own password.
- **AI.** Reuters found agencies using AI chat tools, even though OnlyFans’ terms say you can’t use an AI chatbot to write chats or DMs.



### The lawsuits


The lawsuits: a 2023 class action in federal court in Illinois against OnlyFans’ parent company named one agency and sought more than $5 million; a 2024 class action in California targeted OnlyFans and nine agencies; and 404 Media reported another Illinois class action in 2025. OnlyFans’ parent has said in court that creators, not the platform, are responsible for their content. We haven’t seen rulings in these cases; check the court records for current status.

OnlyFans told Reuters it is “not affiliated with and does not endorse any third party or agency,” and told the BBC it can’t review contracts made off the platform.

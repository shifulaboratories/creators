**The short version:** a good agency grows your page and you can see every dollar. A bad one takes your account, your payouts and your freedom to leave. The difference is almost always in the contract and in who holds the keys.

Managers demanded full logins, changed passwords, emails and bank details so payouts went to them, lied about earnings, and wrote fines into contracts for leaving early. Some creators described threats and pressure to make more explicit content. OnlyFans said it doesn’t endorse third-party agencies and can’t review contracts made off its platform.

## Before you sign

1. **Check them out.** Ask for creators you can talk to directly, not screenshots of earnings.
2. **Do the math.** At a 50% commission, they have to double your sales for you to break even. Run your numbers in the [calculator](/paywall/compare/).
3. **Get it reviewed.** A lawyer reading a contract costs far less than a bad one. See [Redline a contract](/deals/redline-a-contract/), [Agencies and commissions](/deals/agencies-and-commissions/) and [Manager, agent or lawyer?](/team/manager-vs-agent-vs-lawyer/).
4. **Keep the keys.** Your login, your email, your phone number, your bank. Turn on two-factor and keep the codes yourself. See [Account security](/protect/account-security/).

## If you’re already stuck

- **Take back your account first:** change your password and email, reset two-factor, and check your payout details.
- **Download your statements** and the platform’s earnings history before anything changes.
- **Report it** to the platform’s support team. If you’re being threatened, report it to the police; OnlyFans told the BBC it encourages creators to do both.
- **Talk to a lawyer** before you pay any “exit fee.” Penalty clauses aren’t always enforceable.

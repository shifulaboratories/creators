**The short version:** most fan platforms take the same 20%, so the fee is rarely what should decide it. What you’re allowed to post, how the name lands with your audience and future brand partners, and which tools you need (paid messages, locked posts, a shop) matter more.

## Four things that matter more than the fee

1. **Brand fit.** Brands vet where your link in bio goes. A Fanfix or Patreon link reads as a fan club. An OnlyFans or Fanvue link reads as adult to many brand teams, even if your content isn’t. 
2. **Exclusivity rules.** Fanfix’s creator terms say you can’t use your Fanfix account to promote competing platforms, including Patreon and OnlyFans. Fanvue’s terms ban taking payment outside the platform. Read the rules before you cross-promote. 
3. **Who owns the fan.** Patreon lets you export members. Fan platforms built around DMs generally don’t give you fans’ emails, so build an email list or a broadcast channel you control too.
4. **Discovery.** None of these platforms are built to recommend you to strangers the way TikTok or YouTube do. Your social accounts are the funnel, so a platform your followers already trust converts better. 

## Set a simple price

Start with one subscription price, then add extras. Fanfix lets creators charge from $5 to $50 a month, per TechCrunch’s 2023 reporting. A common pattern is a low monthly price that’s an easy yes, with paid messages and locked posts for the fans who want more.

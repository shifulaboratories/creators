**The short version:** nobody publishes a real market price for creator posts, but a few companies publish ranges. They disagree a lot, which is useful to know: if one brand calls your rate crazy, another report says it’s normal. Use the rate check below to see every range that covers your size, start your quote near the middle, and price usage rights, ads and exclusivity as separate lines.

<RateCalculator />

## Why the ranges disagree, and why that helps you

- **The bars disagree, and that’s the point.** Influencer Marketing Hub prints an Instagram nano post at $10 to $100. Later prints a nano Reel at $200 to $500. Neither publishes how it got there. The real market is wide, so there’s room to ask.
- **Smaller deals are the norm.** On Collabstr’s marketplace, nearly 80% of 2025 deals cost under $300, and the average brand paid $193 on Instagram, $186 on TikTok and $255 on YouTube. Those averages mix creators of every size.
- **Asking isn’t getting.** Collabstr’s creators listed YouTube work at $311 on average and were paid $255. Expect brands to counter.
- **TikTok buys views.** Later notes TikTok rates are usually based on your average views, not followers. If your views beat your follower count, lead with views.

Other things that justify a higher number, even without a published percentage:

- **A niche brands fight over.** Finance, beauty, tech and parenting audiences are expensive to reach any other way.
- **Engagement and saves.** Later puts typical engagement at 1% to 5%. If you’re well above it, show it.
- **Production.** Locations, props, hair and makeup, a second shooter, a script the brand needs approved.
- **Exclusivity.** You’re being paid to say no to other brands. Price it as the deals you’d turn down.
- **Rush.** Later calls anything under two weeks a rush.

## Check your quote three ways

1. **From benchmarks (above).** Fast, and defensible when a brand pushes back: “published rates for my tier are X to Y.”
2. **From your views.** Take the median views of your last 10 posts on that platform (not your best one) and decide what you charge per 1,000 views. It keeps your price honest as your reach changes. Set the number from your own past deals; we don’t publish one because no source we trust does.
3. **From your time.** Add up the hours to plan, shoot, edit and revise, and what you need per hour. Use it as a floor: never quote below it.

When the three disagree, quote the highest one you can defend, then negotiate. The [negotiation playbook](/deals/negotiation/) has scripts for the counter.

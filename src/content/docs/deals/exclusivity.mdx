**The short version:** exclusivity is the brand paying you not to work with someone else. It’s normal and often fair, but it’s a separate thing you’re selling. Shrink it to named competitors and a clear end date, then charge for whatever it still makes you turn down.

## Shrink the circle first

Three questions decide how much an exclusivity clause costs you: **who** is off limits, **where** (only sponsored posts, or any mention), and **for how long**. SAG-AFTRA’s checklist asks for a contract that says which brands are off limits and for how long. Revision Legal adds that it should only cover brands that directly compete with the specific product, and shouldn’t apply at all to a UGC-only deal.

## Price what it still blocks

Once the circle is as small as it’ll go, price what’s left. Enter the deals you’d normally do inside it.

<ExclusivityFloor />

If the brand won’t pay the floor, trade instead of discounting: a smaller circle, a shorter tail, or exclusivity only on the platform the campaign runs on.

<DeepDive title="Related terms you’ll see" hint="Right of first refusal, first look, non-compete">
- **Right of first refusal:** before you sign with a competitor, you have to offer the brand the same deal first. Lighter than exclusivity, but it can slow every competing deal down. Give it an end date too.
- **First look:** you show the brand new opportunities in the category first. Lighter still.
- **Non-compete:** sometimes used loosely for exclusivity. If it limits work beyond sponsored posts, or lasts long after the deal, have a lawyer look at it.
- **Category exclusivity on a retainer:** for long ambassador deals, exclusivity is often the main thing the brand is paying for. Price the retainer with it in mind.
</DeepDive>

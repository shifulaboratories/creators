**The short version:** you don’t have to wait for brands to find you. Pitch brands you already use, with one specific idea and one number that proves people watch. ## Who to pitch

- **Brands already in your content.** You have proof you use them, and your audience already knows it.
- **Brands paying creators like you.** Look for paid-partnership labels on posts from creators in your niche and size. If they sponsor someone with your audience, they have a budget for it.
- **Brands with a moment coming.** Launches, seasons, back to school, holidays. Pitch six to eight weeks ahead, when campaigns are being planned.
- **Brands’ own creator programs.** Many brands run an application page or a creator program. Search “[brand] creator program” or “[brand] ambassador.”

Skip brands you wouldn’t recommend to a friend. Your audience’s trust is what every future deal is priced on.

## After they reply

- **They ask for your rates:** don’t send a price list. Ask for the brief and budget, then quote. See [pricing](/deals/pricing/) and the [negotiation playbook](/deals/negotiation/).
- **They offer free product only:** fine if you want it, but it’s still a relationship you have to [disclose](/deals/disclosure/), and it’s not payment. You can say “I’d love to try it. For a dedicated post my rate starts at $X.”
- **They send a contract:** run it through the [redline walkthrough](/deals/redline-a-contract/) before you sign.
- **No reply after a follow-up:** move on and try again next season. Budgets change every quarter.

## Watch out for fake brand emails

Scammers pose as brands to get creators to download “contracts” that steal logins, or to pay for “starter kits.” Check that the sender’s email domain matches the brand’s real website, never download executable files, and never pay a brand to work with you. See [scams that target creators](/protect/scams/).

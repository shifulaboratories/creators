**The short version:** brands expect you to negotiate, and their first offer usually leaves room. Know your number before you reply, quote first when you can, never cut your price without cutting what they get, and get every term in writing. The scripts below cover the six moments creators get stuck on.

## When the offer is low, trade

Cutting your price teaches the brand your rate was never real. Keep the rate and change what they get.

<BeforeAfter
  illustration
  before={{ title: "“Sure, I can do $600 instead of $1,000.”", note: "Same Reel, same rights, 40% less. Next time they'll start at $600." }}
  after={{ title: "“For $600 I can do a 3-frame Story set, organic only, no usage rights.”", note: "Your rate stays $1,000 for a Reel. They pick what fits the budget." }}
/>

Things you can trade instead of price:

| Trade | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Lighter deliverables** | Stories instead of a Reel, one post instead of three |
| **Fewer rights** | Organic only, no ads, a shorter usage window |
| **Better terms** | 50% up front, Net 15, fewer revisions, no exclusivity |
| **More business** | Three posts at a per-post discount, all three in the contract |

## Know when to walk away

Walk away, politely, if a brand won't move on any of these:

- **They own your content** outright.
- **Usage forever** (“in perpetuity”).
- **Paid ads from your handle** with no fee.
- **Category-wide exclusivity** with no fee.
- **You wouldn't recommend the product** to a friend. Your audience's trust is what every future deal is priced on.

<DeepDive title="The six rules in detail" hint="Why each one works">

**Ask for the budget first.** Many brands set a budget per creator before they email you. Asking costs nothing, and sometimes the budget is higher than you'd have quoted.

**Anchor with “starts at.”** If you quote first, give a number at the top of what you can defend. It sets the range and leaves room for packages.

**Price rights as their own lines.** Usage rights and paid ads are where brands get the most value and creators most often give it away. Later's 2026 guide puts usage at 30% to 50% on top of the base rate, and Instagram whitelisting at another 30% to 50%. Exclusivity has no published benchmark, so price it as the deals you'd turn down.

**Get it in writing.** A call is fine for talking, but every agreed change goes into the contract or a follow-up email. In New York City and State, freelance work of $800 or more must be under a written contract that names the pay date.

</DeepDive>

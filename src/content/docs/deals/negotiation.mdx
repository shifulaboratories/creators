**The short version:** brands expect you to negotiate, and their first offer usually leaves room. Know your number before you reply, quote first when you can, never cut your price without cutting what they get, and get every term in writing. The scripts below cover the six moments creators get stuck on.

### 1. Know your number before you reply

Run the [rate check](/earn/pricing/) for your platform and size. Published ranges are wide: an Instagram micro creator’s post is $100 to $500 in one report and $500 to $1,500 in another. That spread is your negotiating room, and it’s your answer when a brand says your rate is “above market.”

### 2. Ask for the budget first

Many brands have a budget per creator set before they email you. Asking for the brief, timeline and budget costs nothing, and sometimes the budget is higher than you’d have quoted.

### 3. Anchor with “starts at”

If you quote first, give a number at the top of what you can defend and say “starts at.” It sets the range for the conversation and leaves room for packages.

### 4. Trade, don’t discount

When the offer is low, keep your rate and change what they get: a Story set instead of a Reel, no usage rights, a later posting date. Discounting teaches the brand your rate was never real. Trading keeps it intact. Things you can trade:

- **Fewer or lighter deliverables.** Stories instead of a Reel, one post instead of three.
- **Fewer rights.** Organic post only, no usage or ads, shorter usage window.
- **Better terms instead of more money.** 50% up front, Net 15, fewer revisions, no exclusivity.
- **More business.** A three-post package at a per-post discount, with all three committed in the contract.

### 5. Price rights as their own lines

Usage rights and paid ads are where brands get the most value, and where creators most often give it away. Later’s 2026 guide puts usage rights at 30% to 50% on top of the base rate, and Instagram whitelisting at another 30% to 50%. Exclusivity has no published benchmark, so price it as the deals you’d have to turn down.

### 6. Get it in writing

A call is fine for talking, but every agreed change goes into the contract or a follow-up email. In New York City and State, freelance work of $800 or more must be under a written contract that names the pay date. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the law</span>

## Know when to walk away

<span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span> Walk away (politely) when a brand won’t move on owning your content, perpetual usage, unpaid ads from your handle, or a category-wide exclusivity at no fee. Also when you wouldn’t recommend the product to a friend: your audience’s trust is the asset every future deal is priced on. A short “Thanks so much, this one isn’t a fit, I’d love to work together on a future campaign” keeps the door open.

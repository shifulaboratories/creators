**The short version:** a brand deal is a small business contract. The brand buys three separate things: the content, your audience’s attention, and (often) the right to reuse your content. Price each one separately, get the terms in writing before you film, disclose the ad clearly, and invoice the day the post goes live.

## Reply to the first email like this

Most brands open with “What are your rates?” Answer with questions. Their answers tell you what to charge.

<CopyBlock
  label="Your first reply"
  text={`Hi [name], thanks for reaching out. I'd love to hear more.

So I can put together the right quote, could you send:
- The deliverables (which posts, which platforms)
- Your timeline and go-live dates
- Whether you'd like usage rights or to run it as a paid ad, and for how long
- Any exclusivity you need
- The budget you have in mind

Talk soon,
[you]`}
  note="Swap in your own voice. Check the sender's email domain first: fake brand emails are a common phishing trick."
/>

## Price it as what it really is

A brand that wants to reuse your video or run it as an ad is buying more than a post. Quote each piece separately so you get paid for each.

| What they buy | What it means | How to price it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deliverables** | The posts themselves: a Reel, a TikTok, a YouTube integration | Your base rate |
| **Usage rights** | The brand reposts or reuses your content on its own channels | +30–50% |
| **Paid ads** | Whitelisting, Spark Ads or partnership ads: your post runs as an ad, often from your handle | +30–50% |
| **Exclusivity** | You don't work with competitors for a window | Price what you'd turn down |
| **Extras** | Raw footage, extra revision rounds, rush delivery, link in bio | Line items |

Even a small deal needs a written agreement. This checklist follows SAG-AFTRA's list of what every influencer agreement should cover.

Something missing? The [redline walkthrough](/deals/redline-a-contract/) and [clause library](/deals/clause-library/) show what to ask for instead. In New York City and State, freelance work worth $800 or more (alone or with the same client over 120 days) must have a written contract, and without a named pay date you must be paid within 30 days of finishing.

## Post it, label it, invoice it

- **Disclose in the video itself**, not only the caption: say “ad” or “sponsored,” and use the platform's paid-partnership label too. US law puts this on you, not just the brand. [Disclosure guide](/deals/disclosure/).
- **Invoice the day it goes live** with the agreed terms, any purchase order number, and links to the live posts.
- **Put the due date in your calendar.** If it's late, follow up politely once, then firmly. [Getting paid](/deals/getting-paid/) has the scripts.

**A pitch that works** is short: who you are and your audience in one line, why this brand fits (proof you use it), one concrete content idea, your recent numbers, and a link to your media kit. Full template: [Pitching brands](/deals/pitching-brands/).

**Your media kit** is one page, updated monthly. Brands skim it in 20 seconds.

- **Who follows you:** top countries, age range and gender split from your analytics.
- **Reach that's real:** median views per post on each platform (not your best post), engagement rate, saves and shares.
- **Proof:** two or three past partnerships with results, if the brand allows you to share them.
- **What you offer:** formats you make, plus “usage rights and paid ads available.” Leave prices for the quote.

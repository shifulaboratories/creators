**The short version:** most late payments are paperwork problems: a missing purchase order number, the invoice sent to the wrong inbox, or a vague due date. Fix those in the contract, invoice the day your post goes live, and follow up on the due date. If you’re in New York City or New York State, the law gives you real leverage.

## Payment terms, decoded

| Term | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Due on receipt** | Pay when the invoice arrives |
| **Net 30 / 60 / 90** | Due 30, 60 or 90 days after the invoice date (or whatever date the contract names) |
| **50/50** | Half on signing, half on delivery or posting |
| **Pay-when-paid** | The agency pays you only after its client pays it. A red flag: you could wait forever |
| **Late fee** | Interest or a flat fee on overdue amounts. Only enforceable if it’s in the contract |

Aim for a deposit on signing with new brands, the balance Net 30 from posting, and a late fee written into the contract. See [payment terms in the clause library](/deals/clause-library/#clause-payment).

<li><strong>A week late</strong><span>Copy accounts payable. Ask for a payment date in writing.</span></li>
<li><strong>Two to three weeks late</strong><span>Reference the contract and the late fee. Pause new work for them.</span></li>

## The law on your side (New York)

New York City and New York State both have a Freelance Isn’t Free Act. If you’re an independent contractor working for a New York client, they’re worth knowing: <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the law</span>

<figure class="sc-figure">
<div class="sc-stats">
<div class="sc-stat is-jade"><span class="sc-stat__value">$800</span><span class="sc-stat__label">Work worth this much, alone or with the same client over 120 days, needs a written contract</span></div>
<div class="sc-stat is-champagne"><span class="sc-stat__value">30 days</span><span class="sc-stat__label">Payment deadline after you finish the work, if the contract names no pay date</span></div>
<div class="sc-stat is-rose"><span class="sc-stat__value">2×</span><span class="sc-stat__label">Double damages for late payment, plus attorneys’ fees</span></div>
</div>
<figcaption>NYC Department of Consumer and Worker Protection; NY General Business Law Article 44-A, in effect statewide since Aug 28, 2024</figcaption>
</figure>

- The written contract must list the work, the pay, and the date you get paid. The state law also requires both parties’ names and mailing addresses.
- It’s illegal for the client to retaliate, for example by blacklisting you, for using these rights.
- In NYC, you can file a complaint with the Department of Consumer and Worker Protection (online or through 311). The hiring party has to respond within 20 days.
- Other places have similar rules, and more are being added. Search your city or state plus “freelance worker protection.”

:::caution[Not legal advice]
This is a summary for creators, not legal advice. Check the current rules where you and the brand are based, and talk to a lawyer before you file a claim.
:::

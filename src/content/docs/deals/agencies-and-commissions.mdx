**The short version:** on most bigger deals, a few people take a slice before the money reaches you. The brand’s agency takes its fee out of the brand’s budget. Your manager (usually 15–20%) and any agent (usually 10%) take theirs from your fee. Each cut can be worth it, but they stack, so know what everyone takes, and on what, before you sign anything.

A friendly agency rep is still paid by the brand. That’s fine, it’s their job, but it means the number they open with is the brand’s number. Brands in the ANA’s 2026 survey said only about half had full visibility into what creators were actually paid, and on average 30% of their influencer spend went to agency services.

## What each one takes

| Who | Typical cut | Taken from | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Brand’s agency** | 10–20% on top of creator fees (Later); 30% of spend on average (ANA survey) | The brand’s budget | Later 2026, ANA 2026 |
| **Manager** | “10% to 20% or more with 15% to 20% being the norm” | Your gross fee | Pfeiffer Law |
| **Talent agent** | Around 10%; SAG-AFTRA caps its franchised agents at 10% with no extra fees | Your gross fee | SAG-AFTRA, LexisNexis |
| **Lawyer** | 5–10% if on a percentage; many charge flat or hourly instead | Your gross fee | LexisNexis |
| **Business manager** | Typically 5% of income | Your income | Armanino |

**How they stack:** cuts are usually each calculated on your gross fee, not one after another. On a $10,000 fee, a 20% manager and a 10% agent leave you $7,000, before taxes and your own costs.

## Negotiating with an agency in the middle

1. **Ask who the client is.** “Is this for [Brand] directly or through an agency?” If it’s an agency, the brand’s full budget is bigger than the number you’re hearing.
2. **Ask for the brief and budget in writing** before you quote. Agencies often have a set budget per creator.
3. **Quote the same rate either way.** The agency’s fee is their business, not a discount on yours.
4. **Price rights as separate lines.** Agencies often bundle usage and ads into the base fee. Unbundle them: see the [rate check](/deals/pricing/).
5. **Check who signs and who pays.** If the agency signs, make sure the contract says when the agency pays you, not “when our client pays us.” See [getting paid](/deals/getting-paid/).

## When your manager negotiates for you

Your manager should send you every offer, the terms, and their recommendation, and you should approve every deal in writing. Ask to see the contract before signing, even if they handle the paperwork. Their 15–20% is for getting you better terms than you’d get alone. The [break-even math](/team/when-to-get-a-manager/) shows how much a manager has to grow your income to pay for themselves.

**The short version:** “redlining” just means marking up a contract with the changes you want before you sign. You don’t need legal training to do the first pass. Read it in five passes (money, rights, exclusivity, control, getting out), propose replacement wording instead of only deleting things, and send it back in one friendly email.

:::caution[Not legal advice]
Educational information only. For a contract that matters, have a lawyer review it.
:::

### Before you start

- **Read it all once without editing.** Highlight anything you don’t understand.
- **Compare it to the email thread.** Fees, deliverables and dates often don’t match what was agreed. The contract wins, so fix it there.
- **Search for red-flag words**: “perpetuity,” “sole discretion,” “work made for hire,” “including but not limited to.” The [clause library](/deals/clause-library/#red-flag-words-to-search-for) has the full list.

### How to mark it up

1. **Use suggestion mode.** In Google Docs use Suggesting; in Word use Track Changes. For a PDF, list your changes by section number in the email.
2. **Replace, don’t just delete.** “Delete Section 5” starts an argument. “Change ‘in perpetuity’ to ‘for 90 days on Brand’s owned channels’” is easy to say yes to.
3. **Keep a comment on each change** with a one-line reason: “Usage is priced separately, see quote.”
4. **Prioritize.** Decide which changes you’d walk away over (usually ownership, perpetual usage, unpaid ads) and which you can trade.

### Sending it back

Group your changes in a short, friendly email. Brands send redlines all day, and a calm list of reasonable asks reads as professional. The [negotiation playbook](/deals/negotiation/#script-redline) has the script.

## When to stop and hire a lawyer

<span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span> A big fee, a term longer than a few months, anything about your likeness, voice or AI, an ambassador or equity deal, or a clause you still don’t understand after reading it twice. A one-off review is usually cheaper than one bad clause.

**The short version:** if a brand gave you anything (money, free product, a discount, a commission, a trip) and you post about it, US law says you have to tell people, clearly, where they’ll see it. The FTC says the responsibility is yours, not only the brand’s. Disclosing doesn’t hurt good content, and hiding it can hurt you and the brand.

## When you have to disclose

The FTC’s guide for creators says to disclose any “financial, employment, personal, or family relationship with a brand.” That includes: <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the law</span>

- **Paid posts** and brand deals of any size.
- **Free or discounted products**, even if the brand didn’t ask you to post.
- **Affiliate links** and discount codes that pay you.
- **Personal relationships**: the brand belongs to a friend, your partner or your family.
- **Tags, likes and pins** can count as endorsements too.
- It applies **even if your review is honest**, and even if you post from outside the US, when it’s foreseeable US followers will see it.

## By format

| Format | What works |
| --- | --- |
| **Reels, TikToks, YouTube videos** | Say it near the start and put it on screen. The platform’s paid-partnership toggle as well. |
| **Stories and photos** | Superimpose the disclosure on the image, and leave enough time to read it. |
| **Livestreams** | Say it more than once, since people join partway through. |
| **Captions and posts on X** | Start with it: “#ad” or “Sponsored by [Brand]” at the beginning, not buried at the end. |
| **Affiliate links** | Next to the link: “I earn a commission if you buy through this link.” |

## What you can and can’t say

The FTC’s guide is also clear about the content of the endorsement itself: <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the law</span>

- **Only talk about products you’ve actually tried.**
- **Don’t say you love something you didn’t.** If you thought it was terrible, you can’t call it terrific because you were paid.
- **Don’t make claims the brand can’t prove**, like a supplement treating a health condition. If a brand gives you claims to repeat, ask what backs them up. The [clause library](/deals/clause-library/#clause-ftc) has wording that puts responsibility for product claims on the brand.

## If a brand asks you not to disclose

Say no, and put it in writing. SAG-AFTRA’s checklist for creator contracts says the agreement should require compliance with the FTC’s endorsement guides, and that disclosures are required even when a product is gifted. A brand that asks you to hide it is asking you to take a legal risk for them.

### 1. Affiliate links

Some tools we recommend may have affiliate programs. If you buy through our link, the company may pay us a commission. You pay the same price.

- **Labeled at the link.** Every affiliate link carries an **affiliate** tag right where you see it, not hidden in a footer. The site adds the tag automatically: affiliate links live only in tool and resource files marked `affiliate`, and those always render with the label and a `sponsored` tag for search engines.
- **A free option, every time.** Every tool pick names a free or non-affiliate alternative. For tools that's enforced in code: a tool file without a named alternative fails the build.
- **Rankings aren't for sale.** Whether a company pays us has no effect on where it ranks, whether it's included, or what we say about it.
- **We'd recommend it anyway.** If we wouldn't tell a friend to use it, it doesn't go on the list, commission or not.

#### Affiliate programs we belong to

None yet. Every program we join will be listed here with the date we joined, and that change goes through a public pull request on GitHub so you can see exactly when it happened.

### 2. Sponsorships

We may take sponsors in the future, for example a newsletter slot. If we do:

- Sponsored content is marked **Sponsored**, at the top, before you read it.
- Sponsors don't get to edit or approve what we write. Negative findings stay in.
- A sponsor's competitors get the same fair treatment in our guides.

### 3. The talent agency

This is the real business, and the reason the site exists. The team behind Select Creators is building a tech-forward talent agency for creators. A useful, honest handbook is how we'd like people to find us.

What that means for you:

- **Nothing is held back.** Every guide is complete on its own. We don't leave out the last step so you have to call us.
- **Conflicts are disclosed.** If a page mentions a creator, brand or company the agency works with, we say so on that page.
- **We don't score points off competitors.** Other managers and agencies are covered with the same standards as everyone else. Our [agency directory](/team/agency-directory/) isn't a ranking with us at the top.

## Why we're this strict

Two reasons. First, in the US the FTC's [Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking) expect paid relationships to be disclosed clearly and close to the recommendation, and we'd rather go further than the minimum. Second, it's the same standard we'd ask you to hold with your own audience. A creator who hides a paid deal loses trust once they're found out, and so would we.

Spot a link that isn't labeled, or a recommendation that looks bought? [Tell us](/about/corrections/) and we'll fix it in public.

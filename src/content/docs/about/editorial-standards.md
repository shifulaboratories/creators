**The short version:** we'd rather say "we don't know" than repeat a guess with confidence. Every claim links to where it came from, stronger sources beat weaker ones, and every page tells you how sure we are.

## The evidence ladder

Not all sources are equal. When two sources disagree, the higher rung wins.

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Published code</strong><span>Code a platform has released, like X's open-source ranking algorithm. It shows what the system does, not what someone says it does.</span></li>
<li><strong>Platform statements</strong><span>A platform's own help center, creator blog, engineering papers or official explainers, with a link and a date.</span></li>
<li><strong>Insiders on the record</strong><span>People who build the platforms, speaking under their own name: a talk, an interview, a published paper.</span></li>
<li><strong>Named practitioners</strong><span>Creators, managers and lawyers who do the work and say it under their own name, ideally with numbers to back it up.</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>Anonymous claims, "a source told me," and screenshots of screenshots aren't on the ladder.</figcaption>
</figure>

On algorithm pages, each claim is tagged **Code**, **Platform** or **Practitioner** so you can see which rung it stands on.

## The labels on every page

The chips under a page title tell you two things at a glance.

**How finished it is**

| Label | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Coming soon** | An outline. The structure is there; the writing isn't. |
| **In progress** | Written and sourced, still being reviewed. |
| **Fact-checked** | Every claim checked against its source by someone other than the author. |

**How sure we are**

| Label | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Sourced** | Backed by code, a platform statement, published reporting or first-hand evidence we've seen. |
| **Creator-tested** | Expert opinion from people who do the work. Useful, but not proof. |
| **Unconfirmed** | A theory, especially about algorithms. We include it because people talk about it, and we say clearly that it's unproven. |

## The rules

- **Link the source, not a summary of it.** If a news story reports on a study, we link the study too when we can.
- **Quote exactly, and briefly.** Quotes are word for word and short enough to leave the original worth reading.
- **Say when a number is old.** Platform rules and payouts change fast. Stats carry the date they describe.
- **No exact numbers without receipts.** Only X publishes its ranking weights. Anyone quoting precise weights for TikTok, Instagram or YouTube is guessing, and we won't.
- **Freshness checks.** Algorithm and tool pages more than six months past their last review get flagged for another look.
- **Case studies need consent or the public record.** We write about creators from what they've published themselves, or with their written OK. Numbers come from them, not estimates.
- **No private individuals.** We don't name people who aren't public figures, unless they've chosen to be named, or the name already appears in court records or major reporting and it matters to the story.
- **Video sources are primary sources.** We embed the original upload from the person speaking, never a reupload or a clip channel.

## Independence and conflicts

- **Money doesn't buy coverage.** Affiliate commissions and sponsors have no say over what we include or how we rank it. Details in [How we make money](/about/how-we-make-money/).
- **Contributors declare conflicts.** If you're paid by, invest in or work for a product you're writing about, you say so in your pull request. That includes our own team.
- **Agency relationships are disclosed on the page.** If a guide mentions a creator, brand or company the agency behind this site works with, the page says so.

## How we use AI

We use AI tools to help research, draft and check pages, the same way many newsrooms do. The rules don't change because a tool was involved: every source is opened and read by a person, every claim must trace to a real source, and a human is responsible for everything we publish. AI never invents quotes, numbers or case studies here. If you find something that looks like it did, [report it](/about/corrections/) and we'll treat it as a serious error.

We also make pages easy to hand to AI: every guide has a plain-text version and an **Ask AI** button, so you can ask follow-up questions about your own situation. AI answers can be wrong, so check anything important against the sources on the page.

## The Underground section

Pages about the gray market (bought followers, paid verification, handle theft) follow extra rules. We cover it as journalism so creators can recognize it and protect themselves. That means published reporting or court records for every claim, no links or handles that help anyone reach a seller, no step-by-step instructions, no affiliate links or sponsors, and a legal review before anything new about a named person or company goes live. The full rules are on the [Underground overview](/underground/).

## When we get it wrong

We fix it in public, with a dated note on the page. See [Corrections and takedowns](/about/corrections/).

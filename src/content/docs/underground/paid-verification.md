**The short version:** before anyone could subscribe to Meta Verified, the blue check was supposed to mean "notable." That made it something people would pay for. The biggest scheme on record, uncovered by ProPublica in 2022, charged clients tens of thousands of dollars and manufactured fake music careers to get them verified. Meta pulled more than 300 badges and banned the people behind it.

## The fake-musician pipeline

In August 2022, ProPublica reporters Craig Silverman and Bianca Fortis published an investigation into what they called "an assembly line for Instagram verification."

## What the platforms did

- **Meta** removed verification badges from more than 300 profiles, banned the main operators and sent cease-and-desist letters.
- **Spotify** removed more than 100 of the fake artists. **Apple Music** removed music too.
- Meta's position, quoted by ProPublica: people should "never pay for verification status because it violates our Terms."

## Why it matters to you

- **Buyers lost both the badge and the money.** A badge bought this way can be pulled at any time, and there's no refund for breaking the terms.
- **Anyone selling a "guaranteed" badge is selling a rule violation.** The legitimate routes today are Meta Verified (a paid subscription that's clearly labeled) or applying for notability-based verification yourself.
- **The same world runs extortion.** ProPublica also described schemes where false reports get an account removed, and then someone sells the "fix." See [Ban and unban services](/underground/ban-and-unban-services/).

## If this happened to you

- **Someone offered you verification for money:** don't pay. Report the account in the app. If you already paid and were scammed, report it at [reportfraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/) (US) and [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/) if it was online.
- **Your badge was removed:** you can reapply for verification through Instagram's settings. There's no back channel, and anyone claiming one is part of the problem.
- **Someone is impersonating you:** report it through Instagram's impersonation form in the Help Center.

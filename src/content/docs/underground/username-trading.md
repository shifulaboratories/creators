**The short version:** a short or one-word handle like @par is rare, so there's a market for them. A lot of the supply is stolen. Reporting from Motherboard, Krebs on Security and The Daily Beast shows the trade runs on SIM swapping, phishing and threats. In February 2021, Instagram, TikTok and Twitter acted against the people at its center.

## A real case: @par

In 2018, Krebs on Security reported that an attacker called several T-Mobile stores until an employee moved security researcher Paul Rosenzweig's phone number to the attacker's SIM. The attacker then took his three-letter Instagram handle, @par. T-Mobile said it was investigating the employee. Instagram later gave the handle back.

## The 2021 crackdown

On February 4, 2021, platforms moved together:

- **Instagram** unmasked people involved, disabled hundreds of accounts and sent cease-and-desist letters demanding a response within 48 hours.
- **TikTok** reclaimed usernames. **Twitter** suspended accounts.

## Why it matters to you

- **Buying a handle breaks every major platform's terms**, and a stolen handle can be reclaimed by the original owner or the platform at any time.
- **Your phone number is the weak point.** If your carrier can be talked into moving your number, anything that texts you a login code can be taken.

## If this happened to you

- **Protect your number:** ask your carrier for a port-out or SIM-change PIN, and use an authenticator app instead of text messages for two-factor login.
- **Your account was taken:** start at Instagram's official recovery page, [instagram.com/hacked](https://www.instagram.com/hacked/). Never pay a "recovery service."
- **You're being threatened or extorted:** keep screenshots, don't pay, and report it to local police and [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov/). If you're in danger, call 911.

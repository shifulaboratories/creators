How the gray market around social media works, and how to protect yourself from it. Journalism only.


These pages explain practices that violate platform rules, and in some cases the law, so creators can recognize and avoid them. **We do not link to, rank or endorse any service described here.**

:::caution[Editorial rules for this section]
- No links, handles, Telegram or Discord names, or prices that would help someone reach a seller. A marketplace is named only when published reporting already names it, and we link to the reporting, not the site.
- No affiliate links, ads or sponsors anywhere in this section.
- No step-by-step instructions. Mechanisms are explained at the level of a news story.
- Every factual claim about a person or company cites published reporting or a court record. New allegations need a lawyer's review before publishing.
- Every page ends with **If this happened to you**: official appeal routes, how to report, how to secure your account, and when to talk to a lawyer or the police.
:::

## In this section

- [Paid verification and badge fraud](/underground/paid-verification/): How verification badges have been sold, as documented by published reporting.
- [Ban and unban services](/underground/ban-and-unban-services/): How mass-reporting and fake claims get accounts removed, and the scams that charge to restore them.
- [Username trading and account swapping](/underground/username-trading/): Why rare handles have resale value, and the risks of buying or selling accounts.
- [Fake engagement](/underground/fake-engagement/): Bought followers, views and reviews, and why they are now a legal issue in the US.

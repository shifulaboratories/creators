## The move: the niche is the audience, not the topic

"I was one of the very few dark-skinned women on YouTube," she told WWD in 2018. She started her channel in 2009, and tutorials for darker complexions were hard to find. That gap made her the person her audience trusted: "Most of my audience comes to me as the authority for dark-skinned beauty. I didn't want to let anyone down," she told Refinery29.

She used that authority in public. Her verdicts on brands with thin shade ranges were blunt. After she said Too Faced's foundation needed more shades, co-founder Jerrod Blandino brought her in to lead an expansion, which Tubefilter called a first-of-its-kind collaboration. "I genuinely looked at everything that existed in the line, and I tried to identify where the gaps were," she said. The darkest new shade sold out within a few days of launch (Fast Company).

## What came after

In August 2020 she told Glossy she was "probably going to be retiring from beauty collabs, because I wanted to end that on a high note." FORVR Mood started with candles and silk pillowcases, outside makeup, so it sold to the same audience without competing with the brands she'd worked with. Her husband, Denis Asamoah, runs operations (Business Insider). WWD reported $700,000 in sales in its first four hours. Later figures vary by source, so we leave them out.

Trust can be lost fast too. In August 2022 the brand named a candle after "Sòro Sókè," a slogan from Nigeria's protests against police brutality. After backlash, Aina pulled it and apologized: "I recognize the gravity of this error" (NBC News).

## What to take from it

1. **Pick a niche by who's underserved, not by topic.** "Beauty" was crowded. Beauty for darker skin had few creators and a lot of demand. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **Honest criticism builds leverage.** She told Refinery29 that being critical had cost her brand relationships. It also made her the person a brand called to fix the problem. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
3. **Turn a collab into proof.** A sold-out darkest shade answered the "deep shades don't sell" excuse with sales. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
4. **Own something next to what you're known for.** Candles let her sell to the same audience without competing with her past partners. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

## The move: independence is the product

A review is only useful if nobody paid for the verdict. Lee built his whole channel on that. "I pay for my food like everybody else," he said after being treated differently at an Atlanta restaurant in 2023. "I'm a normal customer." When people started charging restaurants for reviews in his name, he told IndyStar: "At no point do I want any money from any small restaurant."

The format is simple on purpose. Rolling Stone describes it as a car, a to-go bag and one line: he's got it, let's try it, and rate it one through 10. Viewers know exactly what they'll get, so scores are easy to compare. And he picks a side: "If I can provide the marketing to restaurants who again are trying to survive, I think that's what I truly enjoy doing," he told NBC News.

## What came after

Outlets now call it the Keith Lee effect. Axios reported that 303 In The Cut went from two employees to 21 after his review, according to its owner. His partnerships stay on mission: the 2025 grants program is aimed at small businesses, not at selling products (Entrepreneur).

Reach this big cuts both ways. His fall 2023 Atlanta tour, where he described restaurants turning his family away and then offering him a table once he was recognized, set off a national debate about the city's restaurant scene (Axios Atlanta, Entrepreneur). One Atlanta owner told Axios a low "too salty" score hurt business more than it helped. He has said he never sets out to post a negative review (Entrepreneur, 2025).

## What to take from it

1. **Independence is the asset.** Paying, skipping special treatment and refusing money from the places you review is what makes the verdict worth something. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **Pick who you're for.** Championing small family-run spots gives every video stakes and gives viewers a reason to act, not just watch. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
3. **A fixed format makes every episode comparable.** Same car, same bag, same 1 to 10 scale. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
4. **Big reach has side effects.** A bad score from a trusted reviewer can hurt a small business. Being clear about how you visited keeps you credible when it gets heated. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

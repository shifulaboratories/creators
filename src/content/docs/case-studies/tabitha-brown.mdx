## The move: a personality that travels

In late 2017, Brown was driving for Uber after her disability funds ran out, according to Fortune. On a break she reviewed a Whole Foods vegan sandwich. "And I did a review of the sandwich sitting in my car while I was on my break," she told NPR. Whole Foods got in touch: "And they were like, we saw your video on Facebook."

What turned a lucky video into a career was what came next. She kept showing up on Facebook with recurring shows. When her daughter talked her onto TikTok in March 2020, she brought the same voice, and it worked again. "I've branded myself on just being myself," she told Fast Company.

## What came after

Brands followed. Fast Company lists a YouTube kids' show, Tab Time, a memoir and a cookbook, a McCormick seasoning and Target collections in food, home and clothing. Target's own 2022 announcement says the partnership grew out of influencer work that started two years earlier.

She kept the companies themselves. "We don't owe nobody nothing. We own it 100%," she told AfroTech in 2025. "I don't foresee wanting to get investors, because then you kind of start losing a little bit of your control."

Not everything went to plan. A Los Angeles restaurant she agreed to co-own was called off, and she told Fast Company she hadn't been present enough for it. In January 2025, after Target ended its DEI goals, she posted that a boycott could hurt Black-owned brands on its shelves, while saying she understood people who chose to boycott. theGrio reported that the backlash led her to increase her security. Being tied closely to one retailer means its decisions can land on you.

## What to take from it

1. **The platform you blow up on doesn't have to be the one you stay on.** She went viral on Facebook and grew biggest on TikTok. Her voice was the same on both, so the audience followed. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **A viral video is a door, not a career.** Recurring shows after the first hit are what turned attention into an audience. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
3. **Let retailers distribute, but own the company.** Shelf space at Target and Ulta grew her lines. Bootstrapping kept control with her. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
4. **Spread your partners.** When one retail partner made headlines, she pointed out that her businesses sell in many places. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

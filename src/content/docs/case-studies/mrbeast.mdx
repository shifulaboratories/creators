**The short version:** MrBeast (Jimmy Donaldson) treats the thumbnail as the starting point, watches retention more closely than anything else, refuses to pad videos for length, and got good by making a lot of videos and improving one thing at a time. His team's internal handbook says the same things in more detail.

## In his own words

At VidCon 2022, Donaldson spoke on stage with Todd Beaupré, then YouTube's director of discovery. TechCrunch reported these quotes.

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Thumbnail first</strong><span>He sketches the thumbnail before making the video: "I'd rather have that existential crisis before I make the video."</span></li>
<li><strong>Length follows interest</strong><span>"I've never seen anything that says that videos need to be longer. Just make videos that people want to watch."</span></li>
<li><strong>Reps over talent</strong><span>"When I was a smaller channel, the videos I was making sucked." His advice: "just to make 100 videos and improve one thing each video."</span></li>
<li><strong>Old videos still matter</strong><span>On updating thumbnails of old videos: "That usually does help quite a bit — we do see a bit of an uptick."</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>Quotes as reported by TechCrunch, June 24, 2022</figcaption>
</figure>

He also warned against confusing your audience. "If you watch 100 episodes of 'SpongeBob' and then the 101st is 'Adventure Time,' you'd be like, what is this?" Donaldson said. "The same is true about your audience."

On the same stage, YouTube's Beaupré advised creators to think "audience" whenever they're tempted to say "algorithm." The system follows what viewers do, so optimizing for viewers is optimizing for the system. That's consistent with what YouTube publishes about watch time and satisfaction surveys. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">Platform</span>

## What to take from it

1. **Design the click before the content.** If you can't sketch a thumbnail you'd click, the idea isn't ready. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Practitioner</span>
2. **Find your drop-off point every time.** Open retention for your last video, find the steepest fall, and fix that moment in the next one. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Practitioner</span>
3. **Stop padding.** Neither Donaldson nor YouTube says longer is better. Make it as long as it stays interesting. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">Platform</span>
4. **Volume with intent.** A hundred videos, one improvement each. That's a training plan, not a posting schedule. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Practitioner</span>
5. **Don't copy the scale.** His budgets and team are not the lesson. The process is. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Practitioner</span>

**The short version:** Most creators guess which title, thumbnail or opening will work. The Diary of a CEO built a team whose whole job is to find out. Small wins from a lot of tests added up to one of the biggest podcasts in the world. The same machine also shows the risk of optimizing for attention.

## The move: give failure a job title

"We believe that if we out-fail our competition, we're actually going to out-learn our competition," Bartlett said, as quoted by Inc. in 2026. His company has a Head of Failure and Experimentation, Grace Miller. "Her primary objective is to increase the rate of failure," he told a 2025 conference (BusinessCloud).

In practice that means testing before the public sees anything. Miller told The CEO Magazine the team tries more than 100 creative variations before an episode goes out, and expects most tests to be inconclusive. A Pre-Watch panel of volunteer viewers sees episodes early, and the team cuts where attention drops (Forbes, Colin and Samir). Bartlett told Colin and Samir that rewording a standard subscribe request was credited with about a 300% increase in subscribers.

## What came after

Bartlett told Fortune in October 2026 that he expects about $60 million in podcast revenue this year. He turned down a large network deal that would have meant far more ads per episode: "that is four times more ads than I have to put in now" (Fortune). "If you want to build a durable business in this creator economy, you have to prioritize sovereignty," he said.

Testing everything has costs. In December 2024 a BBC World Service review of 23 health episodes said 15 contained potentially harmful claims. His production company called the reporting "misleading" (The Independent). Since then the show has hired a medical fact-checking team (Fortune) and adds on-screen notes to clarify or correct claims (Irish Times). Critics quoted by Mediaweek argue that endless split-testing pushes thumbnails toward fear.

## What to take from it

1. **Make experimenting someone's job.** Reward running tests, not just winning them, or nobody will try the weird idea. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **Test before you publish.** Show a rough cut to a few people and watch where they drift. You don't need 1,000 volunteers to start. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
3. **Expect most tests to do nothing.** Miller plans for most to be inconclusive. Change one thing at a time so the wins are readable. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
4. **Attention is not the only metric.** A thumbnail or guest that wins the click can cost trust. Decide your limits before the data argues you past them. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

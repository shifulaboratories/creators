**The short version:** Alex Cooper's podcast was owned by Barstool Sports for its first three years. After a public fight over her contract, she left with the show, then licensed it to the highest bidder twice. The lesson isn't "start a podcast." It's that whoever owns the show gets the upside.

## Why ownership was the whole game

At Barstool, the audience she built made money for the company that owned the show. Once she controlled it, the same audience became leverage. Spotify and SiriusXM weren't paying for a microphone and a studio. They were paying for exclusive access to listeners who would follow her anywhere.

Forbes estimated she earned about $32 million in 2024, seventh on its 2025 Top Creators list.

## What to take from it

1. **Read who owns the name before you sign.** The show's title, feed and back catalog are the asset. A salary is not. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **Distribution is a deal, not a home.** She moved from Barstool to Spotify to SiriusXM. The audience came with her each time. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
3. **Once you have leverage, build for others.** Unwell turned one show into a network, though not every signing worked out ([see Alix Earle](/case-studies/alix-earle/)). <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

Before signing anything that touches ownership, read [How to redline a contract](/deals/redline-a-contract/) and get a lawyer.

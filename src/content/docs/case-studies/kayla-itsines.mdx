## The move: proof that multiplies

Bloomberg reported that Itsines started her Instagram in 2012 to share client progress photos, and it reached hundreds of thousands of followers within months. When she sold the program as a downloadable guide, buyers posted their own transformations. Forbes Australia describes that as free advertising, the kind that would cost real money to reach today.

She wasn't sure about going digital at first. "I was hesitant to release the guides because I'm a trainer and I wanted to be there training women in person," she wrote on Sweat's blog in 2025. But the PDF proved demand cheaply, and in 2015 she moved it into a subscription app she and co-founder Tobi Pearce owned. Bloomberg cited App Annie data that it out-earned every other fitness app in 2016.

## What came after

The sale price is a good example of why we date and attribute numbers. A$400 million was widely reported in July 2021 (Startup Daily). Later reporting based on iFIT's filings put it at US$150 million at most (SmartCompany), with Forbes Australia reporting US$40 million of that as cash.

Under iFIT, Forbes Australia reports an $81 million goodwill write-off and a 14% fall in subscription revenue. Then the founders bought it back. "Selling it was the right decision at the time," she told Forbes Australia, and "Buying it back was a no-brainer." She still reads her messages herself: "I'm still in my direct messages – there's 16 million people in there, and I'm in there every single day."

She has also changed course in public. Asked by Bloomberg in 2016 if she regretted the Bikini Body name, she said: "My answer is yes." The program was later renamed.

## What to take from it

1. **Make your audience's results the content.** Proof from real people kept multiplying as more people bought in. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **Use the platform for discovery and your product for the relationship.** Instagram brought people in. The guides and app held the customers and revenue. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
3. **Start with a cheap first product.** A PDF proved demand before she built an app. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
4. **Know what you give up when you sell.** Write-offs and falling revenue followed the sale, and she bought it back two years later. Decide what role you'd keep before you sign. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

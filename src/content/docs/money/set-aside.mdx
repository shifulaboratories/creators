**The short version:** pick a percentage, and every time money lands, move that slice into a separate tax savings account before you spend anything. Four times a year, pay the IRS (and your state) from that account. Do that and April stops being scary.

:::caution[Not tax advice]
The calculator is an estimate using 2026 federal rules. It can't see your deductions, credits or state rules. Use it to plan, then check the real number with a tax pro.
:::

## Find your percentage

<SetAside />

Update it when your income changes a lot, like after a big brand deal or a slow quarter. Overshooting is fine: whatever's left in the tax account after you file is yours.

## Who has to pay quarterly

The IRS expects estimated payments if you expect to owe **$1,000 or more** when you file, after any withholding from a job. For most full-time creators, that's year one. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">IRS</span>

## The safe harbor: how to avoid the penalty

You won't owe an underpayment penalty if your payments (plus any withholding) add up to the **smaller** of these:

<figure class="sc-figure">
<div class="sc-stats">
<div class="sc-stat is-champagne"><span class="sc-stat__value">90%</span><span class="sc-stat__label">of the tax you'll owe for this year</span></div>
<div class="sc-stat is-jade"><span class="sc-stat__value">100%</span><span class="sc-stat__label">of last year's total tax, paid in four equal parts</span></div>
<div class="sc-stat is-rose"><span class="sc-stat__value">110%</span><span class="sc-stat__label">of last year's tax instead, if last year's adjusted gross income was over $150,000 ($75,000 married filing separately)</span></div>
</div>
<figcaption>IRS Estimated taxes page and Form 1040-ES (2026)</figcaption>
</figure>

**Why this matters for creators:** income is lumpy. If you just had a breakout year, paying 100% (or 110%) of *last* year's tax in four parts keeps you penalty-free, even if this year's bill turns out much bigger. You still owe the difference in April, so keep saving your percentage on top.

## How to pay

<figure class="sc-figure">
<div class="sc-compare">
<div class="sc-compare__side is-jade"><h4>Free</h4><ul><li><strong>Your IRS Online Account</strong> (IRS.gov/Account): pay and see every payment in one place</li><li><strong>IRS Direct Pay</strong> (IRS.gov/Payments): straight from a checking or savings account, no sign-up</li><li><strong>EFTPS</strong>: free, but you have to enroll first. Handy once you have a business account</li></ul></div>
<div class="sc-compare__side is-champagne"><h4>Costs extra or slower</h4><ul><li><strong>Debit card, credit card or digital wallet</strong>: the payment processors charge a fee</li><li><strong>Check</strong> with the Form 1040-ES voucher, payable to "United States Treasury"</li><li>Never send cash by mail</li></ul></div>
</div>
<figcaption>Form 1040-ES (2026), "How To Pay Estimated Tax"</figcaption>
</figure>

When you pay, choose **estimated tax (1040-ES)** and the right **tax year**. A payment filed under the wrong year is a common headache to untangle. Your state has its own estimated payments and its own website.

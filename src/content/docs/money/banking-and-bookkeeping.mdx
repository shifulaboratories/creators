- **Business checking:** brand payments, platform payouts and business costs. Nothing personal. This one habit makes taxes, bookkeeping and any LLC protection work.
- **Tax savings:** move your [set-aside percentage](/money/set-aside/) here every time money lands. Pay the IRS from it, and don't touch it otherwise.
- **Personal:** pay yourself a set amount on a schedule, like the 1st and 15th, instead of grabbing money when you need it. Leave a buffer in business checking for slow months.

## Is your money actually protected?

Many slick money apps aren't banks. They partner with a bank, sometimes through a middleman. FDIC deposit insurance pays out if an **insured bank** fails, not if the app or the middleman in between fails.

That's not theoretical. When the fintech middleman Synapse collapsed in 2024, customers of apps built on it were locked out of their money, and a court-appointed trustee found up to **$96 million** of customer funds missing. NBC News reported that the FDIC made clear its insurance fund doesn't cover the failure of nonbanks like Synapse. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">Reporting</span>

**Check before you park money anywhere:** which bank actually holds it, whether that bank is FDIC-insured (look it up on the FDIC's BankFind tool), and whether your money sits in an account in your name.

**How long to keep records:** the IRS's general rule is **3 years** from filing. It's 6 years if you left out more than 25% of your gross income, 7 years for a bad-debt or worthless-securities deduction, and indefinitely if you never filed. Employment tax records (if you run payroll) need at least 4 years. Keep copies of every return. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">IRS</span>

## Credit: build it on purpose

- **Get an EIN** (free from the IRS) and use it on bank accounts and W-9s. It keeps your Social Security number off brand paperwork.
- **A business credit card** for business costs only makes bookkeeping easier, and paying it in full builds a payment history. Most small-business cards still ask for a personal guarantee, so you're on the hook if the business can't pay.
- **Careful with cash advances.** Creator-focused financing that pays you now for future earnings can carry fees that work out far higher than a loan's interest. Read the total cost, not the monthly number.
- Lenders and landlords often ask self-employed people for **two years of tax returns** instead of pay stubs. Clean returns with realistic income help here, which is one reason not to stretch write-offs.

## Retirement: the big legal tax break

When you're self-employed, there's no employer 401(k), but you can open your own. Money you put in can lower this year's taxable income (or grow tax-free later with a Roth version).

A **solo 401(k)** is for a business with no employees other than you (and a spouse). Once it holds $250,000 or more, you file a yearly Form 5500-EZ. Ask your CPA which plan fits before year-end, since deadlines differ.

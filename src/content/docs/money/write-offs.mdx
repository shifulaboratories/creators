**The short version:** a write-off isn't free money. A $100 expense lowers your *profit* by $100, which saves you roughly your tax rate on it, maybe $25 to $40. So only buy things you'd buy anyway, and keep proof for everything you claim.

:::caution[Not tax advice]
These are general US federal rules. An expense that's fine for one creator can be personal for another. When in doubt, ask a tax pro before you claim it.
:::

## The test every expense has to pass

The IRS standard for deducting a business cost is that it's **ordinary** (common in your line of work) and **necessary** (helpful and appropriate for your business). If something is part business and part personal, you deduct only the business share. IRS Publication 334, *Tax Guide for Small Business*, is the plain-language guide. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">IRS</span>

Things that don't count: your everyday rent, groceries, gym, a vacation with one post on it, or clothes you also wear off camera.

**Home office, in practice:** the space has to be used "exclusively" and "on a regular basis" for the business. A desk in the corner of your bedroom that you also game at doesn't qualify. A spare room set up as a studio, used only for filming and editing, can. You pick the simplified method or the actual-cost method each year, and can't switch for that year after choosing.

## Receipts: what to keep

- **Every expense:** the receipt or invoice, how you paid, and one line on what it was for ("Sony mic for podcast set").
- **Car:** a mileage log with date, where you went, why, and miles. An app that logs trips is easiest.
- **Meals and travel:** who you met, what you discussed, where and when. The IRS says a log, diary or notebook works.
- **Gifted products you report as income:** what it was, what it's worth and what was expected in return. See [How creators get taxed](/money/taxes-101/#gifted-products-and-pr-packages).

Keep it all for **at least three years** after you file, longer in some cases. See [Banking, bookkeeping and credit](/money/banking-and-bookkeeping/) for how.

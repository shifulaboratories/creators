**The short version:** get a manager when deals are finding you faster than you can handle them, not when you're hoping someone will find deals for you. A good manager multiplies momentum you already have. They rarely create it from nothing, and at 15–20% of gross they have to earn that cut.

There's no follower count that makes you "ready." The signal is what's piling up in your inbox.

## The usual order

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li><strong>Solo</strong><span>You do everything. Learn how a brand deal works end to end, so you can tell good advice from bad later.</span></li>
<li><strong>First hire</strong><span>An editor or assistant takes production off your plate. Usually the best return on your first dollar. See <a href="/team/hiring/">hiring your team</a>.</span></li>
<li><strong>Lawyer on call</strong><span>Pay per contract. Cheap insurance once deals get real.</span></li>
<li class="is-active"><strong>Manager</strong><span>Inbound deals outgrow your time, and you're leaving money on the table by answering slowly or pricing low.</span></li>
<li><strong>Agent and business manager</strong><span>An agent when you're chasing TV, books or top-tier brands. A business manager when the money gets complicated.</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>A common path, not a rule. Some creators never need a manager.</figcaption>
</figure>

### Signs you're not ready (yet)

- **You want a manager to "get you deals" because there aren't any.** A manager with no inbound to work with is selling hope. Grow first.
- **Someone is pitching you hard and asking for money upfront.** Real managers get paid when you get paid. Upfront representation fees are illegal in California and a red flag everywhere.
- **You can't say what you'd do with the time back.** If the hours would just disappear, the commission is a pure cost.

## Does a manager pay for themselves?

This is the math nobody shows you. A commission comes off the top, so a manager has to grow your income by more than their cut just to leave you even.

<CommissionMath />

At 20%, break-even is +25%. At 15%, it's about +18%. That's very achievable for a good manager who prices you properly and fills your calendar, and impossible for a bad one. The other half of the value is time: if a manager frees ten hours a week and you put them into better content, that compounds.

:::note[What a manager should take a cut of]
Commission is usually on gross income from deals during the term. Ask whether it covers income they had no part in, like AdSense from videos you made before signing, or a deal you closed yourself. Pfeiffer Law calls a commission on net income "much more favorable to an influencer." Read more on [vetting a manager](/team/vetting-a-manager/).
:::

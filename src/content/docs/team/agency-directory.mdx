## What happened to the networks

Multi-channel networks (MCNs) signed huge numbers of YouTubers in the early 2010s, promising growth and ad sales in exchange for a cut of revenue. Big media companies paid hundreds of millions for them. By 2019, Digiday wrote that the term "has become a dirty word."

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li><strong>2014: the gold rush</strong><span>Disney buys Maker Studios for about $675M. Networks sign tens of thousands of channels.</span></li>
<li><strong>2016: the doubts</strong><span>TechCrunch asks "Whatever happened to MCNs?" and calls them "a relic of the past."</span></li>
<li class="is-active"><strong>2017–2019: the cut-backs</strong><span>Disney shrinks Maker to about 300 creators. Defy Media collapses owing creators money. Machinima shuts down.</span></li>
<li><strong>Now: fewer, more focused</strong><span>Survivors focus on rights management, specific niches, or studios. Studio71's North American network was sold to Fixated in 2026.</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>The lesson: a network's promise is only as good as its contract and its balance sheet.</figcaption>
</figure>

## Check a company yourself

- **California agents must be licensed** by the Labor Commissioner. Ask for the license number.
- **Search the company name with "owed" and "lawsuit."** Creators talk publicly when they don't get paid.
- **Ask for current clients you can call.** Then find a former one on your own.
- **Read [Manager, agent or lawyer?](/team/manager-vs-agent-vs-lawyer/)** so you know what each type is allowed to do.

## Get listed, or fix a listing

Listings need a one-line description and at least one dated, public source: reporting, a press release, or a court record. Company marketing alone isn't enough. To add or correct a listing, see [Contribute](/about/contribute/) or [Corrections](/about/corrections/).

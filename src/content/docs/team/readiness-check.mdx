**The short version:** most creators who ask "should I get a manager?" don't need one yet, and the ones who do usually know it because their inbox is overflowing. Tap through five questions and we'll tell you which camp you're in, even when the answer is "not yet."

<ReadinessCheck />

## How we work out your answer

No magic, and nothing hidden. Here's the whole rule:

- **Manager-ready** if brands reach out every week or more, *and* you either spend 5+ hours a week on admin or make $5K+ a month from creating. A manager needs deals to work with and has to earn their cut, so both have to be true.
- **Manager plus specialists** if you're manager-ready and either make $25K+ a month or want TV, books or bigger stages. That's where agents, lawyers and money people each earn their place.
- **Build first** if brands rarely reach out and creating brings in under $1K a month. The content is the job right now.
- **Run it yourself, with backup** for everyone in between: price well, keep a lawyer on call per contract, and make your first hire someone who saves you production time.

Follower count is on the list, but it never decides the answer by itself. What matters is whether deals and admin are piling up faster than you can handle them.

These thresholds are a practitioner's rule of thumb, not an industry standard. If your situation doesn't fit, the guides below go deeper.

## Why agency people are telling you to wait

Because a creator who signs too early, with the wrong person, can end up in a long contract that takes a cut of income the manager had no part in. We'd rather you come back in a year, ready.

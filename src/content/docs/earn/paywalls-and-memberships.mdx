**The short version:** a paywall turns your most loyal fans into monthly income you control. The platform you pick decides how much you keep (from 70% to almost everything) and whether you own your members’ emails. Start with one simple tier, deliver on a schedule you can keep, and pick a platform that lets you take your members with you.

## See what each platform takes

| Platform | Platform cut | Payments | Who owns the list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **YouTube memberships** | You get 70% of net revenue | Handled by YouTube | YouTube |
| **Patreon** (pages launched after Aug 4, 2025) | 10% | 2.9% + $0.30 per US card payment | You can export members |
| **Substack** | 10% | Stripe 2.9% + $0.30, plus a 0.7% billing fee | You can export subscribers |
| **Ghost** | 0% | Your own Stripe, about 2.9% | You own it; flat monthly plan |
| **beehiiv** | 0% | Your own processor | You own it; flat monthly plan |

 Fees as published on each company’s help or pricing pages, checked Nov 26, 2026. Patreon creators who launched before Aug 4, 2025 may be on older plans (5%, 8% or 11%). A few more costs to know:

- **Apple’s cut.** Members who join through an iPhone app’s in-app purchase cost you 30% on Patreon (Patreon adds no processing fee there). US members can join on Patreon’s mobile web checkout instead, which avoids it.
- **Currency and payouts.** Patreon charges 2.5% when a member pays in a different currency from your payout, plus small payout fees (for example $0.25 per Stripe direct deposit).
- **Flat plans.** Ghost and beehiiv charge a monthly plan instead of a cut. The math is simple: a flat plan beats a 10% cut once 10% of your monthly membership revenue is more than the plan’s price.

## Decide what goes behind the wall

Things that work keep fans closer to you without making your free content worse:

- **More of what they love:** bonus episodes, extended cuts, ad-free versions, early access.
- **Access:** a members’ chat or Discord, monthly live Q&A, voting on what you make next.
- **Utility:** templates, presets, recipes, workout plans, the files behind your tutorials.
- **Behind the scenes:** how you made it, your numbers, your process.

Avoid paywalling the thing that grows you. If your free videos get worse, the top of your funnel shrinks and so does the membership.

## Price your tiers

A simple ladder works for most creators:

1. **Supporter** (low price): thanks, a badge, early access. Many fans just want to support you.
2. **Member** (your main tier): the core promise, delivered on schedule.
3. **Inner circle** (high price, limited): direct access, like monthly calls or feedback. Cap the seats so it stays special.

Watch **churn**, the share of members who cancel each month, more than sign-ups. If people leave after month one, the promise isn’t matching what they get.

## Keep the option to move

You can only move members you can contact. On owned platforms, export your member list (emails and tiers) before you switch, announce the move with a date, and give a reason that benefits them. Payment details usually don’t transfer between companies, so expect members to re-subscribe and some to drop off. In-app memberships (YouTube, Instagram, TikTok) can’t be exported, which is the strongest reason to also run an owned tier.

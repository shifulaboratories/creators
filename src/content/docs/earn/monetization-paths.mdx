**The short version:** there are five main ways creators get paid. Platform payouts are the easiest and the least yours. Brand deals pay the most per post but arrive in lumps. Memberships, products and affiliate links grow slower, but you own more of the relationship. The healthiest creator businesses mix at least two, so no single platform or brand can sink a month.

## Platform payouts

The platform shares ad or subscription revenue with you. It’s the easiest money to switch on and the hardest to control.

- **YouTube long-form:** you get 55% of net revenue from ads shown on your videos. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **YouTube Shorts:** ad revenue goes into a creator pool, and you get 45% of your share, which is based on your share of Shorts views. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **YouTube fan funding:** you keep 70% of net revenue from channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers and Super Thanks. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **Getting in:** today, YouTube’s Partner Program needs 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days. YouTube announced that from **Feb 1, 2027** new channels qualify for ad and Premium revenue with 8,000 watch hours in the last 365 days or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days, and Shorts revenue sharing needs 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. Channels already in the program aren’t affected. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

**What it means for you:** payouts reward volume and watch time. They’re a great base, but the platform sets the rate and can change it, as YouTube’s 2027 terms show. Treat them as rent, not salary.

## Brand deals

A brand pays you to make and post content, and often for extra rights: reusing your video, running it as an ad, or keeping you away from competitors. It’s the biggest check most creators get. Published rate data runs from tens of dollars for a nano creator’s post to five figures for creators with a million followers, and Collabstr reports that nearly 80% of the deals on its marketplace cost under $300.

Start with [Brand deals 101](/deals/brand-deals/), price with the [rate check](/deals/pricing/), and read the [clause library](/deals/clause-library/) before you sign anything.

## Memberships and paywalls

A small slice of your audience pays monthly for more: bonus episodes, a community, early access, templates. You keep most of it. On Ghost or beehiiv the platform takes 0% (you pay card processing and a flat plan). Patreon takes 10% for creators who launched after Aug 4, 2025, plus processing. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

**What it means for you:** memberships turn a fraction of your fans into steady income that survives an algorithm change. Compare your take-home in the [paywall guide](/earn/paywalls-and-memberships/).

## Digital products

Courses, presets, templates, ebooks, printables. You make it once and sell it many times. Fees range from 0% on your own Stripe checkout to around a tenth of each sale on hosted storefronts. The constraint isn’t fees, it’s knowing what your audience will pay for. Look for the question your comments ask over and over. See [Merch and products](/stack/merch-and-products/) for tools.

## Affiliate links

You earn a commission when someone buys through your link. It starts working on day one if you already recommend things, and it compounds with search and evergreen videos. Rates are set by each program and change often, so check the program’s own rate card. Always disclose: the FTC counts a commission as a relationship you have to tell people about. See [Affiliate links](/earn/affiliate-links/).

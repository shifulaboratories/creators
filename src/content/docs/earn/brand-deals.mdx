**The short version:** a brand deal is a small business contract. The brand buys three separate things: the content, your audience’s attention, and (often) the right to reuse your content. Price each one separately, get the terms in writing before you film, disclose the ad clearly, and invoice the day the post goes live.

**A pitch that works** is short: who you are and your audience in one line, why this brand fits (proof you use it), one concrete content idea, your recent numbers, and a link to your media kit.

## Your media kit

One page, updated monthly. Brands skim it in 20 seconds.

- **Who follows you:** top countries, age range and gender split from your platform analytics.
- **Reach that’s real:** median views per post on each platform (not your best post), engagement rate, saves and shares.
- **Proof:** two or three past partnerships with results, if the brand allows you to share them.
- **What you offer:** formats you make, plus “usage rights and paid ads available.” Leave the prices for the quote.

## What a brand is actually buying

| Thing | What it means | Price it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Deliverables** | The posts: one Reel, a TikTok, a 60-second YouTube integration, a Story set | Your base rate |
| **Usage rights** | The brand reposts or reuses your content on its own channels | Published benchmark: +30–50% |
| **Paid ads** | Whitelisting, Spark Ads or partnership ads: they run your post as an ad, often from your handle | Published benchmark: +30–50% |
| **Exclusivity** | You don’t work with competitors for a window | No benchmark: price what you’d turn down |
| **Extras** | Raw footage, extra revision rounds, rush delivery, link in bio | Line items |

Benchmarks from Later’s 2026 pricing guide. Use the [rate check](/earn/pricing/) to put numbers on your quote.

## The deal terms to get in writing

Even a small deal needs a written agreement. In New York City and New York State, it’s the law: freelance work worth $800 or more (alone or together with the same client over 120 days) needs a written contract, and if it doesn’t name a pay date you must be paid within 30 days of finishing the work. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the law</span>

SAG-AFTRA’s list of what every influencer agreement should cover is a good checklist: services and deliverables, usage rights (with a time limit, avoiding “in perpetuity”), ownership (they recommend you keep it), pay and payment terms, exclusivity, the approval process and number of edits, who clears music and logos, FTC compliance, and protections against AI use of your voice and likeness.

Before you sign, run it through the [redline walkthrough](/deals/redline-a-contract/) and the [clause library](/deals/clause-library/).

## Disclose every paid post

US law puts disclosure on you, not just the brand. The FTC’s rules for creators, in short: disclose any payment, free product or relationship; put it where people can’t miss it (in the video itself, not only the caption or a “more” link); say it plainly, with words like “ad” or “sponsored”; and use the platform’s paid-partnership label as well as your own, not instead. Full guide: [Disclosing sponsored posts](/deals/disclosure/).

## Getting paid

Send the invoice the day the post goes live, with the agreed terms, the contract’s purchase order number if there is one, and links to the live posts. Put the follow-up date in your calendar. When it’s late, follow up politely once and then firmly. [Getting paid](/deals/getting-paid/) has the invoice checklist and late-payment scripts.

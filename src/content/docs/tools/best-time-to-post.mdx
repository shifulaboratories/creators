**How it works:** pick your time zone, add up to three places your audience lives with their share, and mark the hours people there are usually online. The planner shows, in your own time, the hours that reach the most of them.

## Why there’s no universal best time

You’ll find a lot of “best time to post” charts online. Most come from averages across millions of accounts, which tells you very little about yours. Feeds today pick posts for each viewer from what they like, so a good video can keep finding people long after you post it. That’s why we call one-size timing rules a weak lever in [algorithm myths](/algorithms/myths/).

Timing still matters in two cases. First, when something only works live or early: a live stream, a YouTube Premiere, a Story that disappears in 24 hours, a drop. Second, when your audience is spread across time zones, and 9 PM for you is 4 AM for a third of your followers. This planner is for both.

## Use your own numbers

The online hours above are a starting guess. Swap in what your analytics actually show. On YouTube, open YouTube Studio, then Analytics, then Audience, and look for **When your viewers are on YouTube**, which shows the last 28 days. Then test: post at your top hour for two weeks, then at the next one, and compare views after the same number of hours. More on running tests like this in [Growth experiments](/grow/experiments/) and on picking a pace in the [posting schedule planner](/tools/posting-schedule-planner/).

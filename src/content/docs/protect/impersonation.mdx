**The short version:** fakes exist to scam your fans, usually with "you won a giveaway, DM me" or an investment pitch. Screenshot the fake, report it yourself through the platform's impersonation route (your report carries the ID check, fan reports don't), and tell your audience what you'll never ask them for.

## The evidence kit

Keep one folder per fake. Platforms ask for some of this, and you'll want it if a lawyer or the police get involved.

1. **The profile:** screenshot plus the exact URL and @handle. Display names can change in seconds; handles are what reports use.
2. **The harm:** DMs or comments it sent your fans, ideally with fans' permission to share them.
3. **Your proof:** a photo of your government ID (only to the platform's form), and links to your real accounts.
4. **Dates:** when you first saw it and when you reported it.

## Stop the next one

- **Pin your handles.** A highlight or pinned post: "My only accounts are @… on Instagram, TikTok, YouTube. I will never DM you about prizes, investments or payments." <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
- **Filter comments.** Most platforms let you hide or hold comments with chosen words or links. Add your own name's lookalikes and words like "WhatsApp" and "Telegram".
- **Get verified the official way** where you qualify. It makes the real account easy to spot. Never pay a third party for a badge: see our reporting on [paid verification](/underground/paid-verification/).
- **Watch for deepfakes.** Scammers have already used an [AI-generated video of YouTube's own CEO](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/youtube-warns-of-ai-generated-video-of-its-ceo-used-in-phishing-attacks/) to phish creators. If someone makes an AI fake of you, report it through the same impersonation routes.

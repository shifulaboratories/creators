**The short version:** read the notice, appeal through the app the same day, and keep it short and factual. Appeals have deadlines and some have a limited number of tries. Nobody outside the platform can unban you.

## Pick your platform

## How strikes work, side by side

| | Strikes expire | What ends the account | Appeal window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **YouTube** | 90 days (Community Guidelines and copyright, with Copyright School) | 3 strikes in 90 days, or one severe violation | Termination: 1 year, 2 tries |
| **TikTok** | 90 days | Enough strikes in the window, or one severe violation | Appeal from the notice |
| **Instagram** | Shown in Account Status | Repeated or severe violations | Shown on the disabled-account notice |
| **X** | Not published as a strike count | Severe or repeated violations | Appeal form |

Only YouTube and TikTok publish their strike windows. For Instagram and X, the notice you see is the source of truth.

## Writing an appeal that gets read

1. **Name the rule they cited** and say plainly why your content or account doesn't break it. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>
2. **Stick to facts.** Dates, links, what you posted. Leave out anger and follower counts.
3. **If you were hacked, say so first** and recover the login before appealing, so the platform sees you back in control. YouTube says appeals for hacked channels may fail if recovery isn't complete. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
4. **If it was a mass-report**, say that the reports came in a burst and include screenshots of anyone organizing it.

## Don't dig the hole deeper

- **Don't open a new account to get around a ban.** Platforms treat it as evasion, and it can make the original ban permanent.
- **Don't buy an "unban".** People selling restorations are either scamming you or breaking the rules in ways that can cost you more. See our reporting on [ban and unban services](/underground/ban-and-unban-services/).

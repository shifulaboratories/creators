**The short version:** almost every creator account takeover starts with a message that looks like business: a sponsorship, a copyright notice, a message from "the platform". The tell is nearly always the same. It wants you to **log in through its link, run a file, or hand over a code.**

<ScamDeck />

## The 5-second check

Before you tap anything in a DM or email, run through these. One "yes" means stop.

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Does it rush me?</strong><span>"24 hours or your account is deleted." Platforms give notice in the app with real deadlines, not countdowns in DMs.</span></li>
<li><strong>Does it want a login?</strong><span>Real notices don't need you to sign in through a link. Open the app yourself instead.</span></li>
<li><strong>Does it want me to run something?</strong><span>No brand deal requires installing software to read the contract.</span></li>
<li><strong>Does it want a code?</strong><span>A 6-digit code is a key to your account. Nobody legit asks you to read one back.</span></li>
</ol>

</figure>

## How big is this?

When Google's Threat Analysis Group broke up one campaign aimed at YouTube creators in 2021, it had identified about 15,000 attacker accounts and blocked 1.6 million phishing messages. The lure was fake sponsorship offers. Stolen channels were resold for $3 to $4,000 or used to stream crypto scams. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

The pattern hasn't changed since, only the costume. In 2025, YouTube warned creators about private videos with an AI-generated version of its CEO announcing fake monetization changes. In 2026, scammers filed fake copyright claims against Instagram creators and demanded ransoms to withdraw them.

## Brand deal or bait?

<div class="sc-compare">
<div class="sc-compare__side is-jade"><h4>Signs of a real deal</h4><ul><li>Comes from the company's own domain, and you can find that person on its site or LinkedIn</li><li>A brief and a contract you can preview without installing anything</li><li>Happy to jump on a video call</li><li>Pays through normal invoicing</li></ul></div>
<div class="sc-compare__side is-rose"><h4>Signs it's bait</h4><ul><li>Gmail or lookalike domains, or a brand you can't find</li><li>"Download our app or game to review", or a zipped or password-protected contract</li><li>Wants you to log in somewhere to "accept the campaign"</li><li>Asks you to pay a fee to get paid</li></ul></div>
</div>

## If you already clicked

1. **Change the password** of the account the link was about, and of your email, from a device you trust.
2. **Log out every other session**: each platform's playbook in [Hacked or locked out](/protect/hacked/) shows where.
3. **Ran a file?** Treat the computer as infected. Cookie-stealing malware takes your logged-in sessions, so a new password alone may not be enough. Sign out of all sessions, scan or reset the computer, then change passwords from a clean device. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Security practice</span>
4. **Report the message** in the app it came through, so it gets taken down for the next person.

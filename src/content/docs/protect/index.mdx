**The short version:** your account is your business, and almost every way to lose it has a free, official way back. Move fast, use only the platform's own forms, and never pay a stranger to fix it.

## The three rules when it's on fire

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Screenshot everything</strong><span>Notices, fake profiles, DMs, emails, dates. Appeals and reports go faster with receipts.</span></li>
<li><strong>Official routes only</strong><span>The app, the help center, the form. Never a link from a DM, and never a paid "recovery" service.</span></li>
<li><strong>Tell your people</strong><span>Post from another account or platform so fans don't fall for whatever the hacker or fake posts next.</span></li>
</ol>

## Why creators get targeted

A creator account is worth money to someone else. Google's Threat Analysis Group found hijacked YouTube channels being sold for $3 to $4,000 depending on subscriber count, or used to stream crypto scams to the creator's own audience. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

That's why the attacks look like business: a sponsorship offer, a copyright notice, a message from "the platform". The [scams guide](/protect/scams/) shows what each one looks like.

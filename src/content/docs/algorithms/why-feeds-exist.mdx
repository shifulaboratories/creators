**The short version:** an ad-funded feed makes money when people spend time in the app and come back tomorrow. Every ranking decision serves that. So the posts that win are the ones that keep someone watching, and the ones that pull other people in. That's why a send is worth more than a like.

## Follow the money

Meta is the clearest example because it reports the numbers to investors every quarter.

<MoneyLoop />

You don't have to like this to use it. The feed isn't judging your art. It's predicting whether showing your post makes someone more likely to stay and return.

- **Shares are the growth engine.** A send puts the app in front of someone who wasn't looking at it, and starts a conversation that brings both people back. Instagram's head, Adam Mosseri, said sends per reach correlate with overall reach "more than anything else" in his experience. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **Long-term users matter more than one session.** YouTube surveys viewers and counts time on videos they rate 4 or 5 stars as more valuable, because regret drives people away. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **Rage is capped by penalties.** X's code gives a report a weight of −234 against 0.708 for a like. Content that makes people leave or mute you is expensive. <span class="sc-tag is-code">From the code</span>

## Read your first ring

No feed shows a new post to everyone at once. It's shown to some people, the system watches what they do, and if they react better than expected it's shown to more. Think of it as rings.

<SnowballFlow />

Instagram turned the first ring into a feature: **Trial Reels** are shown to non-followers first, and after about 24 hours you can see their views, likes, comments and shares before deciding whether to show the Reel to your followers. You can also have a Trial Reel shared automatically if it performs well in its first 72 hours. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>

**The short version:** every platform rewards the same three things in slightly different proportions: people **watching**, people **sending**, and people **coming back**. Likes are the cheapest signal everywhere. Negative feedback is expensive everywhere it's published.

- **Instagram:** Mosseri's January 2025 answer: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends," with sends "slightly more important" for reaching people who don't follow you. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **TikTok:** its strongest example signal is whether someone finishes a longer video. Follower count and past hits aren't direct factors. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **YouTube:** watch time, weighted by whether viewers say they were glad they watched. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **Facebook:** Feed predicts whether you'll like, comment on, share or watch each post, and weights those predictions by what people say in surveys is "worth their time." <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **X:** a copy-link share is weighted 20, a reply or DM share 5, a like 0.708. <span class="sc-tag is-code">From the code</span>
- **LinkedIn:** its 2026 feed model is judged on time spent and on likes, comments and reshares, and since 2020 it has scored down posts people are predicted to scroll straight past. <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>

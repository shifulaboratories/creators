**The short version:** a feed keeps a map. Every viewer has a spot on it, and so does every post. To fill your feed, it grabs the posts closest to your spot, ranks them, and shows the best. Every watch, send and swipe nudges your spot, and the map updates within the hour or faster. Your job as a creator is to make each post easy to place, so it lands near the people who'll love it.

## Play the feed

You're a brand-new account. React to a few posts and watch your spot move, your shortlist change, and your feed fill up with what you showed interest in. Every fourth card is something new, on purpose.

## Know the four things it's doing

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Put everyone on the map</strong><span>The model learns a position for every viewer and every post from what people engaged with. Posts that the same kind of people enjoy end up close together.</span></li>
<li><strong>Shortlist what's nearby</strong><span>From millions or billions of posts, it pulls the few hundred closest to you. YouTube calls this candidate generation, Instagram calls it retrieval.</span></li>
<li><strong>Learn from every reaction</strong><span>New reactions update the model fast: hourly for Instagram Explore, at minute-level intervals in ByteDance's published system.</span></li>
<li><strong>Mix in something new</strong><span>Feeds deliberately show things outside your usual spot so they don't narrow into a bubble, and so new posts get a chance.</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>The shape described by YouTube (2016), Instagram Explore (2023), ByteDance (2022) and TikTok (2020)</figcaption>
</figure>

- **The map is learned from people, not just topics.** Instagram's engineers train one model for viewers and one for posts so that a viewer's position ends up "close to the embeddings of relevant items." Nobody hand-labels the map; engagement draws it. <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **Shortlisting is a nearest-neighbor search.** YouTube's 2016 paper describes the step as winnowing "millions" of videos down to "hundreds," using a nearest-neighbor lookup. Instagram's Explore pulls "hundreds of relevant items from a media pool of billions." <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **It updates fast.** Instagram says it can "re-train (fine-tune) our models every hour." ByteDance's Monolith paper syncs new learning to the live system "with a minute-level time interval," because a viewer's interest can "shift their zeal every next minute." <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **New accounts start from a guess.** TikTok invites new users to pick interests like pets or travel; those who skip get "a generalized feed of popular videos" until their reactions take over. <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span>
- **Exploration is a feature.** TikTok says it will "intersperse diverse types of content" and generally won't show two videos in a row from the same creator or sound. YouTube's researchers trained on all viewing, not only recommended videos, because otherwise "it would be very difficult for new content to surface." <span class="sc-tag is-platform">From the platform</span> <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>

## Make your posts easy to place

Your post gets a spot on the map too, and the first people who react to it help decide where. If the first seconds make it obvious who it's for, the right people react and the feed finds more of them. That's our read of how these systems work, not a rule any platform published. <span class="sc-tag is-practitioner">Creator-tested</span>

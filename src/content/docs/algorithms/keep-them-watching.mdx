**The short version:** after the tap, the feed's main question is "did they stay?" Instagram names watch time as a top-three signal, TikTok calls finishing a longer video a strong one, and YouTube counts watch time weighted by whether people say they were glad they watched. So the goal isn't a longer post. It's a post where nobody wants to leave, and that leaves people satisfied.

YouTube's version matters most for long videos: it surveys viewers, and "only videos that you rate highly with four or five stars are counted as valued watchtime." Padding a video to make it longer adds minutes people don't value.

## Use the five-beat structure

This is how most posts people finish are built, whether they last 15 seconds or 20 minutes. It isn't a platform rule. It's the shape that keeps giving people a reason to stay.

<figure class="sc-figure">
<ol class="sc-steps">
<li class="is-active"><strong>Hook</strong><span>Show the most interesting thing, or the question, in the first seconds.</span></li>
<li><strong>Stakes</strong><span>Why this matters to the viewer. Who it's for, what could go wrong, what they'll get.</span></li>
<li><strong>Build</strong><span>Every beat raises a new small question before answering the last one. No dead air.</span></li>
<li><strong>Payoff</strong><span>Deliver what the packaging promised. Clearly, and earlier than feels natural.</span></li>
<li><strong>Next</strong><span>Point to what's next: part two, the series, the reply you're about to make. This is how people come back.</span></li>
</ol>
<figcaption>Our framework, drawn from how the creators in our case studies structure videos. Creator-tested, not a platform rule.</figcaption>
</figure>

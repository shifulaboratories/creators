**The short version:** "the algorithm" is mostly millions of people voting with their thumbs. A feed shows your post to a few people, watches what they do, and shows it to more people if they react well. So going viral isn't a trick. It's four things viewers do, in order: they **stop** for it, they **stay** until the end, they **send** it to someone, and they **come back** for more. Every major platform measures some version of all four.

Most "algorithm secrets" online are guesses dressed up as facts. Every claim in this section traces to one of these, strongest first:

1. **Published code.** What the system actually computes. X is the only major platform that publishes its feed's ranking code.
2. **The platforms' own research.** Papers and engineering write-ups from their machine learning teams, plus independent academic studies. See [the research behind feeds](/algorithms/the-research/).
3. **The platforms' own explainers.** Help centers, engineering blogs, newsroom posts. Accurate, but high level.
4. **Platform employees on the record.** Engineers and product leads answering creators' questions.
5. **Practitioners with a track record.** Strategists and creators speaking under their own name. Labeled "Creator-tested."

Anonymous tips, "I tested this" threads and screenshot charts don't make the cut. Made-up examples are labeled as such.

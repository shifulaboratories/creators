**The short version:** you don't have to guess how feeds work. YouTube, Meta, ByteDance, LinkedIn and Twitter have all published papers and engineering write-ups about their own systems. Below is what each one built, what it found, and the one thing it means for you. Every card links to the original.

## Use what the papers agree on

- **Finishing beats clicking.** YouTube moved off click-through ranking because it promoted videos people didn't finish. <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **Negative reactions cost you.** Instagram subtracts "see less," and Facebook demotes topics people mark "not interested." <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **Your first viewers train the model.** ByteDance built its system to learn from reactions in real time, and LinkedIn tests new posts on a small group first. <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>
- **Ranking changes outcomes.** Twitter's randomized test showed a ranked feed and a time-ordered feed give the same posts different reach. <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>

## The formula Instagram published

Instagram's engineers wrote the Explore score out in plain terms in 2023:

<figcaption>Expected value = W<sub>click</sub> × P(click) + W<sub>like</sub> × P(like) − W<sub>see less</sub> × P(see less) + … Engineering at Meta, Aug 9, 2023</figcaption>

That one line explains most good advice: earn the actions with plus signs, and never give people a reason for the minus sign. <span class="sc-tag is-research">From the research</span>

<DeepDive title="How to read a paper like this" hint="What papers can and can’t tell you">

- **Papers describe a system at one moment.** The 2016 YouTube paper is the foundation, not today's exact model. We pair each one with the platform's current explainer in its [platform guide](/platforms/).
- **Weights are rarely published.** Only X publishes its ranking code and weights. See [Inside X's open-source algorithm](/algorithms/x-open-source/).
- **A finding is not a hack.** These papers say what the system tries to predict. The move is to make things people genuinely watch, like and share, not to game a number.

</DeepDive>

Scroll any big creator or new song and you’ll find accounts that aren’t the artist: clip pages, fan accounts, meme and theme pages that keep using the same song. Some are real fans. A growing number are run by the artist’s team, label or agency.

There are three kinds, and the difference matters:

<div class="sc-stats">
<div class="sc-stat is-jade"><span class="sc-stat__value">Fan pages</span><span class="sc-stat__label">About you: your clips, your quotes, your moments. Run by fans, or by your team.</span></div>
<div class="sc-stat is-champagne"><span class="sc-stat__value">Theme pages</span><span class="sc-stat__label">About a vibe or topic (90s TV, gym, cooking) that uses your song or features you.</span></div>
<div class="sc-stat is-amethyst"><span class="sc-stat__value">Your own extras</span><span class="sc-stat__label">Second accounts you run in the open: behind the scenes, a podcast, a niche.</span></div>

## See how the music industry does it

Music marketers have turned this into a playbook. Billboard reported in August 2025 on labels and agencies using “burner pages and volume posting”: posting a song across many accounts at once. Dazed spoke to a social media manager who runs a “90s culture” TikTok for a label, posting “vibe” content set to new songs, and to an agency founder whose team built Buffy the Vampire Slayer fan accounts to play clients’ music. “We are in an arms race of content output, whether we like it or not,” he said.

It also shows the cost of hiding it. In 2026, Wired reported that the agency Chaotic Good Projects had put clients’ songs on posts from “hundreds to thousands” of TikTok accounts, including for the band Geese. When that came out, Dazed reports, it sparked outrage; the agency took client names off its site, and the band ended up answering “industry plant” claims in interviews. “People are really scared that they are not sure what’s real or not anymore,” the agency founder told Dazed.

Extra accounts are one of the best ways to get the [repeat exposure](/strategy/multiple-impressions/) that turns viewers into fans. Paying others to post clips is the other: see [how clipping works](/strategy/clipping/).

<DeepDive title="The platform rules, in their words" hint="">

**TikTok.** “You can set up multiple accounts on TikTok to create different channels for authentic creative expression, but not for deceptive purposes.” Fan accounts are allowed “as long as they are disclosed in the account name” (the display name, not the @username). Not allowed: “operating networks of accounts that represent similar entities or post similar content,” accounts run in bulk or with unauthorized automation, fake engagement, and using multiple accounts “to intentionally bypass our rules or their enforcement.” TikTok says it spots coordinated networks by accounts “operated by the same entity,” using “the same devices,” or spreading the same narrative. It also says a group of people authentically working together is fine.

**Instagram.** You can add and switch between up to 5 accounts in the app. Reposted content without real changes isn’t recommended, and aggregator accounts that repost others’ work 10 or more times in 30 days lose recommendations. Meta’s Community Standards also have a policy against inauthentic behavior.

**YouTube.** Channels can’t be monetized for content that’s “mass-produced, generic, repetitive,” or for clips without significant changes, even with the original creator’s permission. If a channel is demonetized or terminated, using other channels to get around it “could lead to termination of all channels.”

**FTC.** If anyone is paid to post about you, including per view, the relationship must be disclosed in the post itself.
